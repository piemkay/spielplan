# M5.4 — the DNA half: the pack, the trust boundary, the projection

> **This is a proposal, not an amendment.** Decisions **341** and **328, 329** (the last two jointly
> with M5.6) are recorded in
> [`ROADMAP-M5.md`](ROADMAP-M5.md#decisions-the-owner-must-take-numbered-from-321). Only **M5.1**
> edits the spec file.

§8 stage 7 is the only place in this app where an LLM's output becomes a fact, and the ported rule
is one sentence: "**Failures drop, never repaired.**" (`docs/spielplan-spec_v2.1.md:388`). §9 states
the same thing as a design principle — "**The schema is a cost-saving device, not the guarantee —
the guarantee is the validator**" (`spec:412`) — and the corpus expresses it as a module boundary:
`mdc/dna/store.py` imports `adjudication`, `packs`, `similarity` and `vocab`, and **does not import
`mdc/llm/client.py`**.

This milestone is that boundary, plus the two things on either side of it: the pack the quote must
be a substring of, and the projection that turns a title's keywords into second-tier rows. It is
deliberately **the milestone that never calls a provider**, and that is why it can be built and
tested before M5.5 exists.

It is also the one M5 lane that can be built **without M5.3**, because the install already holds
~485,000 review bodies and per-source `title_meta` for ~19,000 titles from the bundle. That is what
makes the parallelism real rather than notional.

---

## 1. Why these are one milestone

**The pack and the verifier are one function read from two ends.** Stage 7's second rule is
"quote-substring-of-pack via `norm()`" (`spec:386-387`). If the pack writer and the quote checker
normalise differently, good tags are rejected — and `mdc/dna/packs.py:72-76` says so in the
requirement voice: *"Every verification path must use this exact function. A verifier that
normalises differently from the pack writer rejects good tags."* Two agents cannot own the two ends
of that.

**The verifier and the adjudication ledger are one rule.** Stage 7's first rule is "term-in-vocabulary
(**after alias repair + adjudication rename**)", so the verifier calls the adjudication reader
before it decides. `mdc/dna/store.py:49` imports `adjudication` for exactly that.

**The projection and the alias map are the same map the verifier repairs against.** `dna_alias`
(`0004_dna.sql:42-47`) is read by stage 7's alias repair and by stage 8's projection. One reader,
written once.

**Depends on:** M5.1, and only lightly — for pack custody in the raw store and for the queue that
drives stages 5, 7 and 8. **Parallel with:** M5.2, M5.3, M5.5. Its one existing-file hotspot
(`db/dna_terms.py`) is touched by nobody else.

## 2. What does not exist today

### 2.1 There is no `norm()` anywhere in the app

`grep -rn "def norm" backend/spielplan/` finds one function and it is `normalise_kinds`
(`db/library.py:67`), which is about `movie`/`series`.

The corpus's is `mdc/dna/packs.py:72-99`: markup stripping (`\*{1,3}`, `_{2,}`, `[/?spoiler]`), a
17-entry punctuation fold table (`:58-65`: six quote characters, three dashes, four space variants,
a soft hyphen, an ellipsis, three apostrophe variants), whitespace collapse, lowercase. Its docstring
carries the measurement that justifies each half (`:76-94`):

> "**Fold at comparison time, do not strip at pack-build time.** The packs carry what their sources
> published … Measured over the 2026-08-25 runs, this was the single most common mechanical failure:
> 7,334 `**` markers and 1,336 `[spoiler]` tags across the library, plus U+2019 in ordinary
> possessives … Folding can only ever merge strings that already differed by punctuation, so it
> cannot admit a quote that is genuinely absent."

Port it **verbatim**, including the fold table, and give it its own module so there is exactly one.

### 2.2 There is no pack builder, and no custody for a pack once built

`mdc/dna/packs.py` (236 lines): `norm` (`:72`), `pack_path` (`:117`), `build_pack` (`:138`), with
caps `MAX_REVIEWS = 60` ("total, across all sources"), `MAX_PER_SOURCE = 12` ("so no single reviewer
culture dominates"), `MAX_CHARS = 1500` (per review), `MAX_PLOT_CHARS = 4000` (`:45-49`), and
round-robin interleaving across sources (`:168-180`). The craft supplement is `mdc/dna/craft.py`
(356 lines).

§8 stage 5 names all of it (`spec:377-381`) including the known gap: *"the RT critic-blurb pool is a
frozen 2020 dataset -> genuinely new releases get thinner sound/visual facets — known gap,
mitigation: wiki sections + metacritic critic excerpts, revisit if coverage measures poor"*. §14 risk
2 (`spec:485`) repeats it and names the measurement: *"measure facet coverage of post-2025 titles"*.

**An unstated dependency the spec does not carry.** `packs.py` writes packs to a *file*
(`pack_path`, `read_pack`). Stage 7 verifies that a quote is a substring of **that title's pack**, so
the pack must be retained and addressable or the verification is not reproducible. Nothing in the
spec says where the app keeps it. §6.6's "review of DNA rejects" (`spec:322`) needs the pack to
explain a rejection at all. Recommended: the pack goes into M5.1's raw store like any other blob and
a `pack_sha` is recorded beside the run — see §5.

### 2.3 There is no verifier

The corpus's is `mdc/dna/store.py:115-229` (`verify_payload`). Its constants are the parts a naive
port loses:

* `TERM_KEYS = ("term","id","term_id","tag")`, `QUOTE_KEYS = ("quote","evidence","span","text")`,
  `SOURCE_KEYS`, `SALIENCE_KEYS = ("salience","weight","level")` (`:55-58`), under the comment
  *"Key aliases seen in the wild. Extending this list is cheaper than losing a whole batch to a
  field name, but every alias must be unambiguous."* This tolerance exists because a strict reader
  caused a quality collapse; it is not sloppiness.
* `REASONS = ("schema","unknown_term","adjudicated","quote_unverified","unknown_title","no_pack",
  "duplicate")` (`:60-61`) — the vocabulary of a rejection, which §6.6's reject review renders.
* `PIPELINE = "library/v1"` (`:67`), recorded per title so *"a run planner can tell tags produced by
  an earlier configuration from current ones — an older pipeline's output is stale evidence, not
  merely older."*

**The app-side target shape is already right.** `dna_tag` (`0004_dna.sql:73-85`) carries
`salience smallint NOT NULL CHECK (salience IN (1,2,3))` — stage 7's third rule as a schema
constraint — plus `confidence real`, `n_sources integer`, `provider text` ("which LLM produced it
(§6.6 parallel mode)") and `UNIQUE (title_id, version, term, provider)`, the parallel-mode key.
`dna_evidence` (`:91-99`) has `quote NOT NULL` under the comment *"a tag without its quote is
unfalsifiable"* and §4.1 rule 1 makes it binding.

### 2.4 The alias map is loaded, read by nobody, and carries three fidelity gaps

`dna_alias` (`0004_dna.sql:42-47`) is `(version, alias, term)`. `grep -rn dna_alias
backend/spielplan/` returns the loader (`importer/dna.py:181-220`) and `backup/movie_data.py:141`.
**No reader.**

Three differences from the corpus's `load_alias_map` (`mdc/dna/project.py:129-160`) that the port
must close, and one caveat about the third:

1. **`kind='lexicon'` rows must never project.** The corpus skips them (`:144-149`) with a measured
   reason: *"the mood twins' keyword surfaces cannot walk projected rows into the register facet
   through the back door (measured: Django and Hostel both inheriting `register.pulp`, cos 0.894)."*
   The app's loader (`importer/dna.py:200-215`) reads only `raw_term` and `vocab_term` and **drops
   the `kind` column entirely**, so lexicon rows are stored indistinguishably from projecting ones.
2. **The map keys on the normalised raw term.** The corpus uses `normalise(r["raw_term"])`
   (`:153`), with its own docstring warning that *"Using a different normalisation here would
   silently drop most of the map."* The app stores `raw_term` verbatim.
3. **Rows whose vocab term is absent are skipped.** The corpus checks `term not in voc` (`:142-143`).
   The app skips only rows where either field is *empty* (`importer/dna.py:209-212`). **Caveat,
   stated because the loader's own comment claims otherwise:** the comment at `:207-208` says
   *"`dna_alias.term` is NOT NULL, so those are a constraint violation mid-transaction rather than a
   row"* — but `dna_alias` has **no FK from `term` to `dna_term`** (`0004_dna.sql:42-47` FKs only
   `version`), so an unadopted term is stored, not rejected. The behaviour is benign today because
   nothing reads the table; it stops being benign the moment stage 8 does.

**Also absent: the projection weighting.** `mdc/dna/project.py` carries `agreement_weight` (`:86`)
and `PROJECTION_CAP = 0.45` (`:106`) applied to terms listed in `projection_capped_v1.txt` — a file
the real bundle ships (`artifacts/dna_vocab/v1/projection_capped_v1.txt`) and that
`models/artifacts.py`'s `VOCAB_FILES` does not name. Decide whether the cap ships; if it does, the
file has to be read.

### 2.5 The projection job is registered with no callable

`worker.py:1052` — `Job("dna-projection", "M5", "acquisition", "<1 s")`: no `run`, no `owner`. Its
budget is a live coverage row (`spec_coverage.toml:999-1012`), struck out of the M2 CPU-budget row
at M4.16 *"so the claim survives as owed instead of reading as measured"*. That row is this
milestone's, and the job's `run` is what closes it.

`importer/dna.py`'s `load_projected` copies the corpus's already-computed `dna_projected` rows out of
`content.sqlite`. **The app cannot project a keyword to a term.** The corpus can, wholesale
(`mdc/dna/project.py`, 375 lines); §8 stage 8 asks for a per-title incremental version — "new code,
same alias map" (`spec:389-390`).

### 2.6 There is no store for a rejected tag, and §6.6 promises a review of them

§6.6 Data: "review of DNA rejects and low-evidence tags" (`spec:322`). §8 stage 7: "Failures drop,
never repaired" (`spec:388`). **A dropped tag that is never written cannot be reviewed.**

`grep -rn dna_reject backend/` returns nothing. And the three manifest tables that might have served
are declined by the importer on purpose (`importer/load.py:370-372`): `dna_annotation` is "curator
working notes; no app surface reads one", `dna_term_signal` is "vocabulary-building telemetry,
superseded by the shipped vocabulary", `dna_exclusion` is "the corpus's own extraction exclusions,
applied before export".

"Low-evidence" is likewise undefined, and §4.1 rule 2 constrains what it can mean: *"`salience`,
`confidence`, `n_sources` are **weights, never filters**. No `WHERE confidence > x` anywhere"* —
enforced by a static guard (`test_landmine_guards.py:56-61`, `:613`) that also catches the Python
equivalent. **Decision 341.**

### 2.7 One fact the spec never states, and the flywheel is unreadable without it

`themes.robots` **is** a real vocabulary-v1 term. `artifacts/dna_vocab/v1/vocab_v1_all.tsv:166`
carries it (582 terms plus a header), split from `themes.artificial_intelligence` in the "gap-fill
v1.1 2026-08-21" pass, with the split's reasoning in its own note column. §8.4's "zero owned titles
carry `themes.robots`" (`spec:402`) is therefore a statement about **coverage**, not about a missing
term — so "breadth" means covering existing terms across more titles, and the apparent circularity
between decision 163's frozen vocabulary and a flywheel that grows breadth does not exist.

State it in this milestone's module docstring, because M5.4 is where somebody first reads the
vocabulary file and wonders.

## 3. Owner decisions this milestone cannot take alone

**Decision 341 — does stage 7 record what it drops, and what does "low-evidence" mean?** Blocks Phase
D and migration `0027`. Evidence in §2.6. Recommended: the verifier writes a
`dna_reject(title_id, run_id, term, facet, salience, quote, rule_violated, at)` row — dropping from
the *tier* is not the same as discarding the observation, and §6.6's promise needs a table.
"Low-evidence" is defined as an **ordering** on the review screen (ascending `confidence` /
`n_sources`) and never as a WHERE clause, which keeps §4.1 rule 2 intact and keeps
`test_landmine_guards.py`'s guard green.

**Decisions 328 and 329 — the flywheel's feed definitions.** Owned by M5.6, but M5.4 supplies the
measurement `thin_facet` needs: per-title extracted-tier facet coverage over the eleven v1 facets.
Build the measurement here as a domain function; do not invent the threshold.

**One smaller question, worth a numbered entry if the owner wants it settled: does the projection
cap ship?** `PROJECTION_CAP = 0.45` over `projection_capped_v1.txt` is a measured tuning of the
corpus's projected tier, the file ships in the bundle, and `models/artifacts.py`'s `VOCAB_FILES`
does not name it. Either read the file and apply the cap (and add it to `VOCAB_FILES`), or project
uncapped and say so. Do not silently do the second while the file sits in the bundle.

## 4. The work, in order

Phases A-C are pure and testable with no database. Phase D is the trust boundary. Phase E is the
projection and the one existing-file touch. Everything lands in a new `backend/spielplan/dna/`
package.

### Phase A — `norm()`, alone, first

**A1. `dna/norm.py`** — `norm()` ported verbatim from `mdc/dna/packs.py:72-99`, with the fold table
and the docstring's measurement preserved as the argument for why it exists. One module, one
function, so `grep -rn "def norm" backend/spielplan/` can only ever find one.

**A2. A characterisation test against the corpus's own behaviour.** Feed it the markup the docstring
names — `**`, `[spoiler]`, U+2019, the four space variants, the soft hyphen — and assert the output.
This is the single most load-bearing function in the milestone and it has no branches; a table test
is the whole coverage.

### Phase B — the pack

**B1. `dna/packs.py::build_pack(conn, title_id)`** — ported, with the four caps at their measured
values and the round-robin interleave. Reads `title_meta`'s plot fields and `review_store.review`.

**B2. `dna/craft.py`** — the craft supplement (wikipedia craft sections), ported. §8 stage 5 names it
and names its known gap; carry the gap into the module docstring so the next person does not
rediscover it.

**B3. Pack custody.** Write the pack into M5.1's raw store and record its sha beside the run. §2.2's
argument: a verification that cannot be reproduced against the text it was made from is not
auditable, and §6.6's reject review needs the pack to explain a rejection.

**B4. Do not strip at build time.** `norm()`'s docstring is explicit that folding happens at
comparison time and the pack stays faithful to what the sources published. A "cleaner" pack breaks
quote verification for every quote an extractor transcribed across markup.

### Phase C — the alias map, read for the first time

**C1. `dna/aliases.py`** — the reader `dna_alias` has never had, closing §2.4's three gaps:
lexicon rows never project, the key is the normalised raw term, and a row whose vocab term is absent
from `dna_term` is skipped.

**C2. The loader change is `importer/dna.py`'s, and `importer/dna.py` is M5.3's file.** Two honest
options: (a) store `kind` and the normalised key at import, which is a one-column migration and a
cross-lane edit; or (b) keep the table as it is and apply all three rules in the reader, which costs
a join against `dna_term` per read and cannot recover `kind` at all, because the loader never stored
it. **(a) is the correct answer and the lexicon column is the reason** — a rule that cannot be
applied because the data was dropped is not a rule. Coordinate the `importer/dna.py` edit with M5.3
rather than taking the file.

**C3. The projection weighting**, per the open question in §3: `agreement_weight`, and
`PROJECTION_CAP` over `projection_capped_v1.txt` if the cap ships.

### Phase D — the trust boundary

**D1. `dna/verify.py::verify_payload(...)`** — ported from `mdc/dna/store.py:115-229`, with the
three rules in §8's order:

1. **term-in-vocabulary**, after alias repair (Phase C) and adjudication rename (D2);
2. **quote-substring-of-pack** under `norm()` (Phase A), against *that title's* pack (Phase B);
3. **salience in {1,2,3}**, which `dna_tag`'s CHECK also holds.

**D2. `dna/adjudicate.py`** — the reader of `dna_adjudication` the verifier calls, ported from
`mdc/dna/adjudication.py`'s `rename()` / `is_retired()`. **This is the ingest-time half of §8 stage
3's two ledgers**; M5.3 owns the derive-time half (corrections). Coordinate: one of you writes
`dna_adjudication`'s reader and the other writes `credit_correction`'s, and neither writes both.

**D3. The key-alias tolerance ships.** `TERM_KEYS`, `QUOTE_KEYS`, `SOURCE_KEYS`, `SALIENCE_KEYS`
(`mdc/dna/store.py:55-58`), with the corpus's own comment quoted: extending the list is cheaper than
losing a whole batch to a field name. A strict reader is not a stricter guarantee; it is a more
expensive one.

**D4. Failures drop and are recorded, never repaired.** Per decision 341: the tag does not reach
`dna_tag`, and a `dna_reject` row carries the term, the quote, the salience and the rule violated —
one of `mdc/dna/store.py:60-61`'s `REASONS`. **No repair path exists at any layer.** A tag whose
term is one character off a real term is dropped, not corrected.

**D5. Every passed tag carries its evidence quote.** §4.1 rule 1 and `0004_dna.sql:91-92` both say
so, and the importer already rejects an extracted tag with no evidence row. The verifier's output
must carry the quote through to the writer, and the writer must refuse a tag without one.

### Phase E — the incremental projection

**E1. `dna/project.py::project_title(conn, title_id)`** — per-title, reading the title's keywords,
mapping through Phase C's alias map, writing `dna_projected` with `via` (the keyword/alias that
produced it) and `weight`. `UNIQUE (title_id, version, term)` (`0004_dna.sql:113`) makes re-running
idempotent if the write is an upsert.

**E2. Give `Job("dna-projection", …)` its `run`** — the registration is `worker.py`'s and
`worker.py` is **M5.1's file**. Export the callable; let M5.1 register it, or land a one-line
registration following M5.1's budget-paragraph convention.

**E3. Scope every read to one vocabulary version.** `db/dna_terms.py`'s `ACTIVE_VERSION` (`:86-89`)
and `active_version(conn)` (`:92-98`) are the one derivation, and §14 risk 7 (`spec:491`) makes it
binding: *"every read is scoped to one version"*. This is the milestone's one existing-file hotspot
and the reason it belongs here.

**E4. Never union the two tiers except through `dna_tagged`.** `0004_dna.sql:120-128` is the
sanctioned view and `test_landmine_guards.py:135` (`SANCTIONED_VIEW`) enforces it. The projection
writes tier 2 and reads tier 1 only through the view.

### Phase F — the thin-facet measurement

**F1. A domain function** giving a title's extracted-tier facet coverage over the eleven v1 facets.
M5.6 turns it into a flywheel feed once decision 329 supplies the threshold. **Do not invent the
threshold here**, and do not name the function `is_thin` — `placement/features.py:90-109` already
owns that name for a different test, and conflating the two is exactly what decision 329 exists to
prevent.

**`0027_dna_extraction.sql`.** Take the number from

not re-litigate that.

**Never edit `0004_dna.sql`.** It is applied, checksummed, and it is where all of this lives.

## 7. Exit criterion, as something to run

> On a real install with the real corpus bundle: a fabricated tag inside an otherwise well-formed
> provider response is absent from the verifier's output, and so is a tag whose quote is not a
> substring of that title's pack under `norm()`, and so is a tag whose salience is outside {1,2,3}.
> **No tag is ever repaired, only dropped**, and every drop is recorded with the rule it violated.
> Every tag that passes carries its evidence quote. A tag whose term the adjudication ledger renames
> passes under its new name; one the ledger retires does not pass at all. A per-title projection of
> one acquired title completes in under 1 s on CPU with no GPU present, and re-running it changes
> nothing.

| # | measure | pass |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | fabricated term, schema-valid payload | absent from output; `dna_reject` row with `unknown_term` |
| 2 | quote not in the pack | absent; `quote_unverified` |
| 3 | quote differing from the pack only by `**`, `[spoiler]` or U+2019 | **passes** — the fold is doing its job |
| 4 | salience 0 and 4 | absent; recorded |
| 5 | four key-alias spellings of `term` and `quote` | all four accepted |
| 6 | adjudication `rename` | passes under the target term |
| 7 | adjudication `drop` | absent; `adjudicated` |
| 8 | any repair path | none exists — assert by reading the module for a write that changes a term |
| 9 | passed tag without an evidence quote | refused by the writer |
| 10 | per-title projection, CPU only | < 1 s |

## 8. What this milestone deliberately does NOT do

* **It never calls a provider.** No adapter, no key, no HTTP to an LLM. That is **M5.5's**, and the
  separation is the point: `mdc/dna/store.py` does not import `mdc/llm/client.py`, and neither
  should this package.
* **It does not merge passes or compute consensus.** Union-merge with agreement-as-confidence
  (`mdc/dna/store.py:246-277`) is **M5.5's**, because the arithmetic is about runs and the runs are
  the LLM layer's.
* **It does not fetch reviews.** Stage 2's eight sources are **M5.3's**; the pack is built from rows
  the bundle already shipped, which is what lets this lane run in parallel.
* **It does not apply `corrections_v1.tsv`.** Credit facts at rebuild are **M5.3's**. This milestone
  owns the DNA-verdict half only.
* **It does not build the reject review screen.** §6.6's surface is **M5.6's**; this milestone gives
  it a table.
* **It does not build the flywheel.** §8.4's queue is **M5.6's**; this milestone supplies the
  thin-facet measurement and no threshold.
* **It does not touch `worker.py`.** M5.1 registers the job.
* **It does not introduce a second vocabulary version.** Decision 163 refuses one, §14 risk 7
  explains why, and every read here is scoped through `db/dna_terms.active_version`.
* **It does not re-project the bundle's existing `dna_projected` rows.** Those came from
  `content.sqlite` through `importer/dna.load_projected`; re-deriving them is a different question
  with its own cost, and §8 stage 8 asks only for the incremental per-title version.

## 9. Risks and gotchas

**`norm()` is one function and it must stay one.** Two normalisations is the failure the corpus's own
docstring is written to prevent, and it fails *silently*: good tags get rejected and nobody sees a
crash. Add a static guard that `grep`s for a second definition if that is cheap.

**Folding is at comparison time, never at pack-build time.** A pack that has been "cleaned" verifies
fewer quotes, not more, and the damage is invisible until somebody counts rejections.

**The key-alias tolerance looks like sloppiness and is not.** `mdc/dna/store.py:53-54` records that
extending the list is cheaper than losing a whole batch. A reviewer who tightens it is undoing a
measured decision.

**§4.1 rule 2 is guarded over Python as well as SQL.** `test_landmine_guards.py:56-61` and `:613`
catch `WHERE confidence > x`, `AND salience = 3`, `n_sources IN (2,3)` and the Python equivalents.
"Low-evidence" must be an ordering.

**Only `dna_tagged` may union the tiers** (`0004_dna.sql:120-128`, `test_landmine_guards.py:135`).
The projection is tier 2; any read of "what DNA does this title have" goes through the view.

**`dna_alias` has no FK from `term` to `dna_term`,** so the table will happily hold a mapping to a
term the vocabulary does not carry — and the loader's own comment says the opposite. Do not trust
the comment; check the row.

**The pack has to be retained or stage 7 is unauditable.** Nothing in the spec says so; §6.6's reject
review is what makes it necessary. Decide it here, in `0027`, or the first rejection an admin looks
at cannot be explained.

**`themes.robots` is in vocabulary v1.** Anyone reading §8.4's "zero owned titles carry
`themes.robots`" as "the vocabulary lacks the term" will conclude the flywheel is impossible. Put the
fact in the module docstring with its line reference.

**Line length 108, ASCII in console output, and comments argue why.** The corpus docstrings being
ported carry their measurements; port the measurements too, or the port reads as an assertion where
the original was an argument.
