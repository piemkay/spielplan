# M5.3 — the sources and the derive: crawl once, re-parse forever, corrections last

> **This is a proposal, not an amendment.** Decisions **326** and **334, 335, 346** are recorded in
> [`ROADMAP-M5.md`](ROADMAP-M5.md#decisions-the-owner-must-take-numbered-from-321). 326 gates this
> milestone's migration, and 346 is a **spec amendment decision 171 already mandated and that never
> landed** — only **M5.1** edits the spec file, so M5.3 supplies the text and M5.1 applies it.

§14 risk 5 is M5's largest named risk and its whole content is this milestone:

> "**Per-title incremental derive is new code** replacing deliberately-wholesale corpus steps —
> port the invariants (raw/derived split, idempotent re-ingest, adjudication- **and
> corrections-**at-derivation — two distinct ledgers: DNA verdicts at ingest, source-credit facts
> at rebuild) or inherit the bugs they were built to kill (the 787-rows-reverted-twice scar)."
> (`docs/spielplan-spec_v2.1.md:489`)

§8 stage 3 states the same rule as a requirement — the derive "ends by applying BOTH curated ledgers
… exactly as the corpus rebuild runs corrections last: a derive that regenerates rows without
re-applying them silently reverts curated fixes (§14.5)" (`spec:369-374`) — and `0004_dna.sql:131-133`
already carries it as a comment over the two tables, verbatim.

**Both tables are loaded and neither has a reader.** `importer/dna.py:765-771` says so in the tree,
in a comment written by M4.9: *"§8 stage 3 — the derive that would apply them — is M5's, and grep
finds exactly two readers of `credit_correction`: this writer and `backup/movie_data.py`."*

This milestone is the eight fetchers, the parsers, the per-title derive, the two ledger appliers and
the reviews gate. Its exit criterion is the scar.

---

## 1. Why these are one milestone

**A fetcher with no parser writes bytes nobody reads; a parser with no fetcher has nothing to read.**
The corpus keeps them in separate modules and they are still one shipment: `mdc/sources/tmdb.py`
imports `rawstore`, `ids`, `queue` and `sources/base`, and `mdc/parse/titles.py` imports `ids` and
`sources/_htmlutil` and **no transport at all**. That separation is worth porting exactly, and it is
a separation *inside* one milestone rather than between two.

**The derive and the ledgers cannot be split, and §14.5 is why.** A derive that lands without its
ledger appliers is a derive that silently reverts curated fixes the first time it runs twice. The
scar is 787 rows reverted **twice**, and the second reversion is the one a plan that ships "the
derive now, the ledgers next" reproduces.

**The reviews gate reads what the derive writes.** Stage 4 is a predicate over
`review_store.review` (`0003_content.sql:239-251`), filled by stage 3. Building the gate against a
table nothing fills is building against a fixture.

**Depends on:** M5.1 — the fetcher, the raw store and the queue it drives. **Parallel with:** M5.2,
M5.4, M5.5. It shares no file with any of them: the source adapters and the derive are new packages,
and its two existing-file hotspots (`importer/dna.py`, `importer/meta.py`) are touched by nobody
else.

## 2. What does not exist today

### 2.1 There is no fetcher for any of §8 stage 2's eight sources

§8 stage 2 (`spec:365-368`) names eight: `tmdb:resolve` then `tmdb:detail`, `wikidata:resolve`,
`omdb:detail`, `trakt:summary` then `comments`, `wikipedia:article`, `tvmaze:show`, `rt:page`,
`metacritic:page` then `reviews`.

The app has exactly one connector. `backend/spielplan/connectors/__init__.py:1` says so: *"Outbound
connectors. Spec v2.1 §7 (Jellyfin); TMDB/OMDb/Trakt/LLM arrive with M5."*

The corpus has all eight under `mdc/sources/`, with line counts: `tmdb.py` 217, `wikidata.py` 223,
`omdb.py` 59, `trakt.py` 133, `wikipedia.py` 183, `tvmaze.py` 47, `rottentomatoes.py` 96,
`metacritic.py` 152, plus `base.py` 129 (the `HandlerSpec` registry, with the `paid` flag) and
`_htmlutil.py` 225. `letterboxd.py` (63) exists there and §8 does **not** list it — do not port it
without a decision.

**Three of the eight already have a credential path.** `connectors/registry.py:88-99` seeds `tmdb`,
`omdb` and `trakt` from env with the comment *"§6.6 configures these in the admin UI at M5; seeding
them here means an automated install can arrive with them already filled in"*, and
`core/config.py:71-74` declares the four settings.
`test_static_contracts.py::test_compose_forwards_every_connector_seed_variable` (`:1041`) holds
compose forwarding all six variables. The other five sources are keyless.

### 2.2 There is no parser at all, and the app has never seen a raw source response

`title_meta` (`0003_content.sql:72-79`) is `(title_id, source) -> payload jsonb` — exactly the shape
stage 2's fetchers write into. But `importer/meta.py` **ingests already-parsed payloads out of
`content.sqlite`**; nothing in the app turns a raw TMDB response, an OMDb blob, a Wikidata entity, an
RT page or a Metacritic page into those rows.

That is `mdc/parse/titles.py` — 1,032 lines and fourteen named entry points:
`parse_tmdb_detail` (`:36`), `parse_omdb_awards` (`:252`), `parse_omdb` (`:284`),
`parse_jellyfin_item` (`:372`), `parse_trakt_summary` (`:453`), `parse_trakt_ratings` (`:481`),
`parse_tvmaze` (`:488`), `parse_wikipedia` (`:559`), `parse_mpst` (`:598`),
`parse_wikidata_entity` (`:666`), `parse_letterboxd_page` (`:782`), `parse_rt_page` (`:807`),
`page_belongs_to_title` (`:933`) and `parse_metacritic_page` (`:991`) — plus `mdc/parse/reviews.py`
(490) and `mdc/ids.py` (347: `upsert_person` `:213`, `classify_role` `:318`, `keep_credit` `:330`).

`page_belongs_to_title` deserves naming: the two scraped sources are matched by slug guesses, and a
page that belongs to a different film is the same silent wrong match `resolve.py:145-179` refuses to
make at the Jellyfin end. Port it with its year tolerance.

### 2.3 `title` has no `wikidata_id` and no `wikipedia_title`

`0003_content.sql:27-59` carries `imdb_id, tmdb_id, tvdb_id, trakt_id, trakt_slug,
letterboxd_slug, rt_slug, metacritic_slug, jellyfin_id` and neither Wikidata nor Wikipedia. §8 stage
2's `wikidata:resolve` and `wikipedia:article` key on exactly those two, and the corpus carries
both. Migration `0026`.

Note that `wikidata:resolve` is the one that "halves guessing" (`spec:366`) — it produces the MC/RT/
Letterboxd slugs the two scraped sources would otherwise guess at. So the ordering inside stage 2 is
not arbitrary: Wikidata before RT and Metacritic, or the scrapers guess.

### 2.4 The two curated ledgers are loaded, stored, and read by nobody

**The tables.** `dna_adjudication` (`0004_dna.sql:135-143`, `PRIMARY KEY (version, term)`, `verdict
IN keep|rename|drop|merge`, re-keyed at `0015_seed.sql:159-166` to add `id`/`scope`/`title_id`/
`quote`/`source`) and `credit_correction` (`0004_dna.sql:145-155`, `+evidence` at
`0015_seed.sql:176`). Both sit under a header comment that is §8 stage 3 verbatim
(`0004_dna.sql:131-133`).

**The loaders.** `importer/dna.py`: `load_adjudications` (`:223-329`) with
`ADJUDICATION_COLUMNS` (`:65`) and a "parses to nothing, do not replace" guard (`:244-266`,
`:302-312`); `parse_corrections` (`:680`) and `load_corrections` (`:727-781`) with
`CORRECTIONS_COLUMNS` (`:69`).

**The gap, stated in the tree.** `importer/dna.py:765-771` and the report line it writes at
`:774-779`: *"{n} credit correction(s) stored for §8 stage 3 (M5) — nothing applies them yet, so no
credit on any card reflects them"*. And: *"Import-time patching of `credit` is deliberately not done
— §8 stage 3 owns that, and a second implementation of it here is how a derive silently reverts
curated fixes."*

The corpus's appliers are `mdc/dna/adjudication.py` (252 lines) and `mdc/corrections.py` (137).
`corrections.py`'s docstring is the design rationale and should be quoted in the port
(`mdc/corrections.py:1-22`): *"a fix typed straight into the database is not a fix: the next rebuild
deletes it. The DNA layer learned this the expensive way … **Applied last, and only to named
rows.**"*

### 2.5 `DELETE FROM credit_correction` is unscoped, and decision 171 already recorded what that costs

`importer/dna.py:759` is `DELETE FROM credit_correction` with no WHERE. Decision 171's Cost paragraph
(`ROADMAP-to-M5.md:390-412`) named the consequence in advance, verbatim:

> "`DELETE FROM credit_correction` is unscoped, so when §6.6's ledger editors land, **an
> in-app-authored correction absent from the next bundle's TSV is wiped** (probed: P4 removed the
> app row)."

Decision 162 makes a models-only re-import the only import that recurs, and decision 171 rules that
all four curated ledgers reload on **every** import including models-only. So an app-authored fix
survives every *re-derive* — which is literally what §6.6 promises — and is destroyed by the next
*re-import*. **Decision 326.**

### 2.6 `resolve_title_fields` is wholesale, and a per-title version does not exist

`importer/meta.py:140-158` groups **every** `title_meta` row in the database
(`SELECT title_id, source, payload FROM title_meta`, no WHERE) and executemanys over all of them.
Its docstring (`:143-148`) already anticipates the M5 collision: it only touches titles that have a
meta row, so it does not blank *"whatever §8's acquisition path wrote for a title the corpus never
shipped"*. A per-title version is one WHERE clause and must not be a second implementation of
`best()` — `meta.best` (`:76-87`) and `SOURCE_PRIORITY` (`:35-38`) are `mdc/export.py`'s ordering
ported verbatim and are the single definition.

### 2.7 The reviews gate has its column and no predicate

`review_store.review` (`0003_content.sql:239-251`) carries `source`, `body text NOT NULL` and a
**generated stored** `word_count` computed from `body`. So "plot + multi-source reviews >= 50 words"
is one query over `word_count` and `count(distinct source)`. And `acquisition_job.retry_after`
(`0005_ledger.sql:139`) is already commented *"§8 stage 4: 30-day review-accrual window"* — and read
by nothing (`grep -rn retry_after backend/spielplan/` is empty).

What is missing is the predicate itself, and it is genuinely ambiguous (**decision 335**): is it
">= 2 sources *and* each review >= 50 words", or ">= 50 words total across >= 2 sources"? And "plot"
is a resolved field with a documented fallback
(`test_title_meta.py::test_the_overview_falls_back_from_plot_full_to_plot_short`), so the predicate
has to name which. The coverage row reads it as ">= 2 sources AND >= 50 words" without saying 50
words of what (`spec_coverage.toml:3206-3207`).

### 2.8 Nothing says which of stage 2's eight sources must succeed

§8's failure rule is "Failure at any stage parks the job with a reason" (`spec:398`); stage 4 is
described as the quality bar. Those read opposite ways for a title where Metacritic 404s but TMDB
succeeds. **Decision 334**, and it is the difference between "unattended" working and not.

## 3. Owner decisions this milestone cannot take alone

**Decision 326 — where the ledger editors write, and what survives a re-import.** Gates migration
`0026` (the `origin` provenance column) and is jointly owned with M5.6, which builds the editors.
Evidence in §2.5. Recommended: rows carry `origin in {'bundle','household'}`; the importer's DELETE
is scoped to `origin = 'bundle'`; the app can export household rows as TSV. §6.6's sentence is
amended to say "survive every future re-derive **and every re-import**", or narrowed to what is true.

**Decision 334 — which stage-2 sources are required?** Blocks the stage-2 driver. Recommended: only
`tmdb:detail` is required, because it is the spine; every other source is best-effort and its failure
is a `detail` note rather than a park; stage 4's reviews gate is the only quality bar, which is what
the clause ordering implies.

**Decision 335 — the exact reviews-gate predicate.** Blocks Phase E. Recommended: `plot_full` or
`plot_short` non-empty, AND reviews from >= 2 distinct sources, AND total review body >= 50 words,
measured on the stored `word_count` (so **before** any normalisation, which is also the cheap
answer). Anything short parks at stage 4 with `retry_after = now() + 30 days` and the counts in
`reason`.

**Decision 346 — decision 171's owed spec amendment.** Decision 171's "Spec amendment" paragraph
(`ROADMAP-to-M5.md:412`) mandates replacing §4.3's `dna_vocab/v1/` bullet and adding a sentence to
§10. Neither is present: §4.3's bullet (`spec:196`) still ends at "applied at every derive, §8 stage
3", and §10 (`spec:427`) carries no "re-applies the curated ledgers it ships" sentence. Under
CLAUDE.md decision 171 is still normative and the amendment is simply **owed**. M5.3 touches exactly
those clauses, so it supplies the replacement text and M5.1 lands it.

## 4. The work, in order

Phases A-C land inside new packages and change no existing file. Phase D is the derive and touches
`importer/meta.py`. Phase E is the gate. Phase F is the two ledger appliers and **lands last**,
because it is the step §14.5 is about and it must be written against a derive that already runs
twice.

### Phase A — the source scaffolding

**A1. `sources/base.py`** — the `HandlerSpec` registry ported from `mdc/sources/base.py`: `kind`,
`fn`, `source`, `requires` (the config capability flag), `default_priority`, `phase`, and **`paid`**
(`mdc/sources/base.py:51-53`, which exists because *"at corpus scale the difference between them is
roughly a hundred euros a click"*). Every adapter registers here; the driver never names an adapter
directly.

**A2. `sources/_htmlutil.py`** — `clean_text`, `ld_json`, `next_data`, `unescape`, `walk`, ported.
It is a dependency of the parsers, not of the fetchers.

**A3. Credentials through the registry.** `tmdb`, `omdb` and `trakt` already have `env_seeds` rows
(`connectors/registry.py:90-99`) and a compose guard. They are read through **M5.5's generic
`ConnectorSpec`** if it has landed, and through a narrow per-source read if it has not. Do not write
a second generic loader — `registry.py` is M5.5's hotspot file.

### Phase B — the eight adapters

**B1-B8.** One module per source, each a thin `HandlerSpec` over M5.1's fetcher and M5.1's raw store.
Port the corpus's request shapes verbatim, including the ordering inside stage 2 (`tmdb:resolve` →
`tmdb:detail`; `wikidata:resolve` before `rt:page` and `metacritic:page`, because it is what supplies
their slugs).

**Every adapter writes bytes and returns nothing else.** The parse happens in stage 3, from the raw
store. That is the raw/derived split and it is the thing §8's "re-parsing is free forever" promises.

**Do not port `letterboxd.py`.** §8 stage 2 does not name it. Note the omission in the plan rather
than quietly adding a ninth source.

### Phase C — the parsers

**C1. `derive/parse.py`** — the fourteen entry points of `mdc/parse/titles.py`, rewritten from
sqlite to Postgres and from a sink-callback to whatever shape the derive wants. **Pure functions over
bytes**: they import no transport, exactly as the corpus's do.

**C2. `derive/ids.py`** — `classify_role` (`mdc/ids.py:318`), `keep_credit` (`:330`),
`upsert_person` (`:213`). `upsert_person` is the one that touches the app's `person_id_seq`
(`0015_seed.sql:25`), so a minted person lands at or above 1e9 by the same rule as a minted title.

**C3. `page_belongs_to_title`** (`mdc/parse/titles.py:933`) ported with its year tolerance, and
applied to both scraped sources. A page that belongs to the wrong film is the scraped-source
equivalent of the name+year mis-match `resolve.py:145-179` refuses.

**C4. `derive/reviews.py`** — `mdc/parse/reviews.py` (490 lines), which produces the rows stage 4's
gate counts. `importer/reviews.py`'s `REVIEW_SOURCE` (`:57-67`) and `repair_mojibake` (`:76-98`)
already exist and are the app-side definitions; reuse them rather than porting the corpus's.

### Phase D — the per-title incremental derive

**D1. `derive/rebuild.py::derive_title(conn, title_id)`** — the single-title version of the corpus
rebuild, keyed `entity_key`, writing `title_meta` / `credit` / `review` / `display`. **New code by
§8's own admission** (`spec:359`) and the subject of §14 risk 5.

**D2. Idempotence is the acceptance test, not a nice-to-have.** Running `derive_title` twice must
produce the same rows. Write that test before the ledger appliers exist, so the appliers are added to
a derive already known to be idempotent.

**D3. A per-title `resolve_title_fields`.** One WHERE clause on `importer/meta.py:150-151`'s query,
calling the same `best()` and the same `SOURCE_PRIORITY`. **Do not fork the resolution rule.**

**D4. `title_meta` per source, kept.** The table is `(title_id, source)` and the whole design keeps
per-source rows so `best()` can resolve per field. A derive that collapses them is a derive that
destroys the thing `SOURCE_PRIORITY` exists to order.

### Phase E — stage 4, the reviews gate

**E1. The predicate**, per decision 335, as one query over `word_count` and `count(distinct
source)`. It reads the generated column, so it costs nothing.

**E2. Park with the counts in the reason.** `acquisition_job.reason` is *"shown verbatim on the admin
board"* (`0005_ledger.sql:138`), so the reason string is a product surface: "reviews gate: 2 sources,
38 words — retry window 30 days" is the shape proposal 109's prototype used and the shape the board
will render.

**E3. The 30-day window is scheduled by the queue's `defer`, and `retry_after` is the board's copy.**
Two writes, one truth; the queue decides when the task leases again and `acquisition_job.retry_after`
is what §6.6 renders.

### Phase F — the two ledgers, corrections last

**F1. `derive/ledgers.py::apply_adjudications(conn, title_id)`** — ported from
`mdc/dna/adjudication.py`. Reads `dna_adjudication` for the active version and the title's scope,
applies `keep | rename | drop | merge`. **At ingest**, which is where §14.5 puts it: DNA verdicts
apply when a tag is written, not at the end of the derive.

**F2. `derive/ledgers.py::apply_corrections(conn, title_id)`** — ported from `mdc/corrections.py`.
Reads `credit_correction` for the title and applies it to `credit` rows. **Last**, after everything
else the derive writes, and only to named rows — `mdc/corrections.py:19-22`: *"Every entry identifies
one title and one claim, carries the evidence that settles it, and is applied by `apply` at the end
of `rebuild`. Nothing is inferred."*

**F3. Two calls at two points, never one merged pass.** The coverage row says it explicitly: *"the
two ledgers are applied at their own points rather than merged into one pass"*
(`spec_coverage.toml:3195`). A merged pass is the shape that reverts things.

**F4. Delete the "nothing applies them yet" report line.** `importer/dna.py:774-779` writes
*"{n} credit correction(s) stored for §8 stage 3 (M5) — nothing applies them yet"*. Once F2 lands
that sentence is false; replace it with the truth rather than leaving a stale reassurance in the
import report. Same for the comment block at `:765-772`.

**F5. `origin` scoping**, per decision 326: the column in `0026`, and `importer/dna.py:759`'s
`DELETE FROM credit_correction` becomes `DELETE FROM credit_correction WHERE origin = 'bundle'`.
`load_adjudications`'s DELETE (`:317`) is already scoped by version and needs the same treatment for
household rows.

## 5. Schema changes

**`0026_acquisition_sources.sql`.** Take the number from
[`ROADMAP-M5.md`'s ledger](ROADMAP-M5.md#the-migration-ledger).

* **`title.wikidata_id`** and **`title.wikipedia_title`** — §2.3. The corpus carries both and this
  schema carries neither, and two of stage 2's eight sources key on them.
* **The `origin` provenance column** on `credit_correction` and `dna_adjudication`, per decision 326
  — `text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'bundle' CHECK (origin IN ('bundle','household'))`. The default makes the
  backfill free: every existing row came from a bundle.
* **Nothing else.** `title_meta`, `credit`, `review_store.review` and `display` already have the
  shapes stage 3 writes into. `acquisition_job.retry_after` already exists.

**Check `backup/movie_data.py` before assuming a restored archive still fits.** `:149-150` names
`dna_adjudication` and `credit_correction` in the archived set; a new NOT NULL column with a DEFAULT
restores fine, but a CHECK added without the DEFAULT would not.

**Never edit `0003`, `0004`, `0005` or `0015`** — all applied, all checksummed, all touching these
tables.

## 7. Exit criterion, as something to run

> On a real install with the real corpus bundle: a title carrying one of the shipped
> `credit_correction` rows is re-derived from its raw documents; its `credit` rows are regenerated
> and **the correction is still there afterwards**, with `adjudications_v1.tsv` applied at ingest and
> `corrections_v1.tsv` applied last, at their own points rather than in one merged pass. Deriving
> the same title a second time changes nothing. A title with a plot but fewer than two review
> sources parks at `reviews gate` with the counts in its reason and a 30-day window, and the admin
> retry resumes from that stage, re-reads the content-addressed raw store instead of re-fetching,

## 8. What this milestone deliberately does NOT do

* **It does not build the fetcher, the raw store or the queue.** All three are **M5.1's**; this
  milestone is their first real user.
* **It does not build the DNA pack, the verifier or the projection.** `norm()`, `build_pack`,
  `verify_payload` and the alias-map projection are **M5.4's**, and stage 3 hands off to stage 5
  without knowing how they work.
* **It does not call an LLM.** Stage 6 is **M5.5's**.
* **It does not build the ledger editors.** §6.6's three editors are **M5.6's**; this milestone
  builds the *appliers* and the provenance column they will need.
* **It does not touch the Jellyfin connector.** `connectors/jellyfin.py`, `resolve.py` and
  `sync/seen.py` are **M5.2's**. Stage 1's identify calls the shipped resolver.
* **It does not port `mdc/sources/letterboxd.py`.** §8 stage 2 does not name it.
* **It does not re-crawl the ~19,000 corpus titles.** The export bundle ships no `data/raw/`, so
  their bytes do not exist and cannot be re-parsed. This milestone's raw store starts empty.
* **It does not rewrite `importer/bundle.py`'s load path.** The bundle importer stays wholesale; §10

* **It does not touch `title_company`'s coarse key**, still skipped at import, still M4.5's open

* **It does not build a per-source admin card.** §6.6's TMDB/OMDb/Trakt cards are **M5.7's**.

## 9. Risks and gotchas

**§14.5's scar is invisible in a happy-path test.** A brand-new acquired title carries no
adjudication and no correction, so "applies BOTH ledgers, corrections last" is satisfied **vacuously**
by code that does nothing. Every test of F1 and F2 must run against a title that *has* a curated row,
and the real bundle supplies six.

**The order of F1 and F2 is the requirement.** Adjudications at ingest, corrections last. Swapping
them, or merging them into one pass, passes any test that only asks "is the correction present" and
fails the one the spec actually states.

**`importer/dna.py:759`'s unscoped DELETE is a live landmine the moment M5.6's editors ship.**
Decision 171 recorded it and accepted it *because nothing wrote household rows yet*. This milestone
is where that stops being true, so `origin` scoping lands here even though the editors are M5.6's.

**`page_belongs_to_title` is not optional.** Two of the eight sources are scraped by slug. Without
the check, a wrong slug writes another film's reviews into this title's pack, and stage 7's quote
verification will happily pass a quote that is a genuine substring of the wrong film's pack.

**`resolve_title_fields` groups the whole database.** Calling it per title without the WHERE clause
turns a per-title derive into a full-library rewrite, silently, on every acquisition.

**Rate limits are real and the two scraped sources are slow on purpose.** `mdc/config.py`'s
`HOST_POLICIES` runs RT and Metacritic deliberately slowly with a long breaker cooldown. Do not
"optimise" the per-host policy to make a test faster; make the test tolerant instead.

**Keep parsers free of transport.** `mdc/parse/titles.py` imports `ids` and `_htmlutil` and nothing
else, and that is why a parser change never requires another crawl. A parser that reaches for the
fetcher breaks the invariant §8's "re-parsing is free forever" rests on.

**Integration tests skip silently without `TEST_DATABASE_URL`.** Most of this milestone is in that
layer.

**Line length 108, ASCII in console output, comments argue why and cite the clause.** `importer/dna.py`
is one of the densest argued files in the tree; a diff that does not match its register reads as
off-convention next to it.
