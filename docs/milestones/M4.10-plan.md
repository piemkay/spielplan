# M4.10 — Rate and Rank: the verdict path and the tier board

> ## DECISIONS TAKEN SINCE THIS PLAN WAS WRITTEN
>
> This plan was drafted while the questions below were still open, so it may present two branches or defer to "an owner decision". **It no longer does.** The rulings are binding; where the plan below offers a choice this section has already made, follow this section. Full reasoning and evidence are in [`ROADMAP-to-M5.md`](ROADMAP-to-M5.md#decisions-taken).
>
> **Decision 175 — What does "a straddling title" mean, when every title on a 900-title board straddles at ±1σ?**  
> Keep the ±z·σ reach — it is the right predicate and it is already one function — and fix the two things that are actually broken: the constant, and the sharing of it. `straddle_z` stays §6.3's badge constant and is retuned from 1.0 to 0.15; Tonight stops borrowing it and gets its own `BOUNDARY_Z = 0.6` beside CAP_PAIRS in tonight/round.py. Reject the posterior-mass redefinition: at the σ the model actually produces it is provably a no-op. Reject narrowing prior_var: measured, it moves nothing.
>

**This milestone is not in §12.** It exists because the two surfaces that write the Personal
Ledger — `rate/session.py` and `api/rank.py` — both guard their writes with a read, then some
work, then a write, on a connection that autocommits every statement; and because both of them
raise *after* the row is durable, so the client is told a write failed that in fact happened, and
retries it into an append-only table with no undo. One agent applying M4.6's advisory-lock helper
to all four seams writes one diff. Four agents write four incompatible transaction shapes into the
same answer handler.

Make every observation write exactly once under a lock, and stop the two surfaces raising after
the row has already committed.

## Why these changes are one milestone

`rate/session.py` and `api/rank.py` are the two files here — sixteen findings and fifteen — and
eight of those are literally the same shape at four seams: the Rank queue seal
(`api/rank.py:329-347`), the Rate card token (`rate/session.py:742-748` against the write at
`:814`), the skip journal append (`rate/session.py:920`) and the tier-set PUT read at
`rank/tiers.py:146` racing the worker's refit at `worker.py:207-221`. Each is a check-then-act on
a bare pooled connection. The project already owns the right idiom and uses it in exactly one
place — `tonight/play.py:46`'s `_FINISH_LOCK = 6202` and the two-int `pg_advisory_xact_lock` at
`:611`, whose comment argues precisely the make-the-loser-wait shape this needs. Four seams, one
idiom, one commit.

The second half is the same story from the other end. `rate/session.py:752-777`'s `_ledger_update`
deliberately catches `(RefitRefused, ValueError)` because "a fit that refuses is a model problem,
not a reason to lose the tap". `api/rank.py:273-276` and `:357-360` call the same function bare.
Two surfaces, one seam, opposite decisions — and the Rank one is a 500 after `drop_rules.drop`
already committed a tier edit and up to two duels. The fix is one shared reporting helper, and the
place to make that call is the milestone that is already inside both files.

`arch-05` and `syncpush-13` sit here rather than with the sync milestone because the edit is
inside `rate/session.py`'s verdict transaction: the Jellyfin round trip is awaited between
`observations.record_verdict` and `_append`, and moving it out is what makes the Rate double-tap
409 reachable at all — the finding measured the window widening to the connector's 15 s timeout.
`observations._set_state`'s own docstring already says the network call "belongs to the route,
after the commit"; this milestone makes that sentence true. And `perf-03` sits here rather than
with the model because the offload happens at the call site, not in `ledger/model.py`, and the
coupling map's dependency edge puts it before `ml01` and the incremental-refit work.

`ti-11`'s four gather-two-requests tests land here for the two routes this milestone owns, because
this is the milestone that makes them go green. Registering them earlier would add failing rows to
the coverage contract with no code to answer them.

**Depends on:**

| milestone | what this one uses |
|---|---|
| M4.6 | the transaction/advisory-lock helper in `api/deps.py` that all four seams take |
| M4.7 | the app-level handler that renders a unique violation as 409 rather than `app.py:114-117`'s blanket 500 |
| M4.8 | the gather-two-POSTs integration shape (two clients over one cookie jar, `raise_app_exceptions=False`), without which none of these races can be asserted |

## What is broken today

Grouped where two findings are one repair. Every line number below was read at the M4.5 head
(`50753a3` plus the uncommitted spec amendments) — several findings were written before the
2026-09-03 amendments and where one has gone stale it says so.

### The four unserialised seams

**1. The Rank queue seal's single-use guard is an unlocked read-then-write.**
[`dd08-rank-queue-answer-double-write`]
`api/rank.py:329` compares `read.answered_comparisons` — a bare `SELECT count(*) FROM duel`
(`rank/read.py:171-182`) — against the sealed `n`, then inserts at `:337-347`, in autocommit, with
two round trips in between and no unique index anywhere on `duel` (`0005_ledger.sql:38-58`) to
backstop it. Four independent probes agree: with no injected latency, two gathered answers under
one seal returned 200/200 and wrote two rows in most races, sometimes with contradictory outcomes
A and B for the same pair, and one of the doubled races drew `uniform_holdout` — §13's only
admitted evaluation data, in a §4.2 append-only table on a surface with no undo. The client makes
it easy to reach: `rank.svelte.js:270` has `if (!rank.pair) return;` and no in-flight guard, where
`rate.svelte.js:253` and `tonight.svelte.js` both have one. **It should** take the lock, re-count
after the winner commits, and leave through the existing 409.

**2. Two simultaneous Rate taps on one card token answer 500, not 409 — and the loser's Jellyfin
write has already gone out.** [`dd08-rate-tap-race-answers-500-instead-of-409`, `ml12-double-tap-500-not-409`]
One repair. `_take_card` (`rate/session.py:742-748`) validates against the snapshot `_resume` read
without a lock; both taps pass; the loser's `INSERT INTO rate_observation` collides with
`rate_observation_seq` (`0011_rate.sql:69`), the transaction rolls back — so no duplicate
observation — and `app.py:114-117` renders the `UniqueViolationError` as `500 {"detail": "database
error"}`. `rate.svelte.js` treats a 500 as a hard error and does not re-read, so the losing tab is
stuck on an answered card until reload. `ml12` refutes the double-write hypothesis and confirms
the status-code one; both are true and both are fixed by claiming the card under a row lock as the
first statement inside the existing transaction. **It should** be §6.1's 409 with a reason, taken
*before* `_push_state` reaches Jellyfin.

**3. `record_skip` appends the journal row outside any transaction.**
[`rrh-lifecycle-07-skip-append-not-transactional-wedges-session`]
`rate/session.py:920` calls `_append` bare; the other four taps wrap it (`:814`, `:880`, `:955`,
`:1022`). `_append` (`:680-738`) is an INSERT at `seq + 1` followed by an UPDATE of `rate_session`.
A process death, a dropped pool connection or a cancelled task between the two leaves the journal
at seq N+1 with the session still at N, and every later `_append` collides forever. Reproduced by
inserting that row: verdict, skip and not-seen all answered 500, `GET /api/rate` kept serving the
same unanswerable card, undo tombstoned the phantom row and the next verdict still 500'd (the
unique index is not partial on `undone_at`), and only `DELETE /api/rate/session` recovered.
Skip is the most frequent tap in a sweep. **It should** be one transaction, like its four
siblings.

**4. `ensure_card` is not idempotent under concurrent GETs.**
[`rrh-lifecycle-09-ensure-card-not-idempotent-under-concurrent-gets`]
`rate/session.py:355` checks `s.current_card is None` on an unlocked read and `stash_card`
(`:412-431`) UPDATEs unconditionally, so two GETs on an empty table both stash — same title, two
tokens, last write wins. `api/rate.py:139-141`'s docstring promises "a second GET returns the same
card under the same token". Reproduced: two tokens, the first verdict 200, the second 409.
**It should** stash with `AND card_token IS NULL` and, on zero rows, re-read and return the card
the other request stashed.

**5. A tier-set PUT that lands during the worker's refit is reverted, and the two kind rows split
to different K.** [`rrh-lifecycle-04-tier-set-sweep-reverts-put-and-splits-kinds`]
`worker.py:207-221` reads `refits_owed`, runs `refit_user`, then calls `clear_refit_request`
(`rank/tiers.py:230-236`) with no timestamp predicate. `refit_user` loads `tier_set` at
`ledger/refit.py:312-314` and, seconds later, upserts `tier_set = EXCLUDED.tier_set` at
`:419-432` unconditionally. Reproduced: PUT K=5 queued a refit, PUT K=9 landed during the fit, and
after the sweep the movie row read K=5 with `refit_requested_at` NULL — the person's K=9 choice
reverted, its refit request gone — while the series row read K=9. Because `rank/tiers.py:115-122`'s
`tier_set_of` reads `ORDER BY kind LIMIT 1` on a comment that says "the movie row is the one asked
because save writes both and they cannot disagree", `GET /api/rank/tiers` then answered 5 labels
and a legal series drop into tier 7 was refused 422 by `rank/drop.py:91-93`. **It should** be
impossible for a fit to overwrite a tier set it did not fit against, and `tier_set_of` should read
the row for the kind it is being asked about.

**6. One failing tier-set refit aborts the whole sweep.**
[`rrh-lifecycle-05-tier-set-sweep-aborts-on-first-failure`]
Same loop, `worker.py:207-221`, no per-item `try`. `refit_user` raises `RefitRefused` on a
non-finite fit (`refit.py:337`). Reproduced: the job raised and all four owed rows stayed owed;
the tick re-runs every 60 s, so the failing user burns a full MAP fit per minute on the loop that
also serves sync and backup, and everyone behind them in `ORDER BY refit_requested_at` starves.
`refit_all` already isolates per user (`refit.py:458-468`, "One person's bad fit must not stop the
others"). **It should** do the same.

**7. Nothing in the suite can see any of this.** [`ti-11-no-double-submit-coverage-anywhere`]
Of 1110 backend tests exactly one uses `asyncio.gather`, and it drives `auth.check_pin` directly
rather than a route. Of 168 coverage rows, "double", "rollback" and "crash" appear in zero. The
row that claims property 1 — `tonight-rank-queue-pair-is-single-use` — rests on a sequential
replay test. `conftest.py:180-185`'s `app` fixture already hands out independent cookie jars, so
the shape costs nothing but the writing. **It should** exist, for the two routes this milestone
owns; the setup/admin and Tonight-join tests belong to M4.6 and the Tonight milestone.

### Raising after the row has committed

**8. Rank's drop and answer routes raise after the observation is durable, and every retry writes
another row.** [`ml07-rank-drop-500-after-durable-observation`,
`rrh-rank-post-commit-exceptions-duplicate-writes`]
One repair, three raise sites. `api/rank.py:273-276` calls `refit.update_incrementally` after
`drop_rules.drop` committed the edit and its neighbour duels in one transaction
(`rank/drop.py:101-118`); `:357-360` does the same after `record_duel`; `:362-369` builds and
records a rail line whose `rail.record` raises `RailError` above `MAX_LINE = 400`
(`home/rail.py:88,166-169`) — and `_payload` at `:202` does it again for the drop's log line.
Reproduced both ways: with `update_incrementally` raising `RefitRefused` the drop route answered
500 with the `tier_edit` durable and the retry wrote a second (1 → 2), while the same refusal on a
Rate verdict answered 200; and with two 260–390 character title names the answer route 500'd after
the duel committed (4 → 5 rows). `rank/tiers.py:56-62`'s `MAX_LABEL` comment closed exactly this
hole for tier labels and names the failure: "a long enough label turned every drop into that tier
into a 500 with the observation already durable, and each retry wrote another". **It should** be
`{"applied": false, "reason": ...}` under the already-gated `ledger` key, and a rail line that
truncates display text instead of raising after the fact.

**9. A Ledger cache miss runs a full MAP fit with a dense `O((p+n)^3)` inverse inside the request.**
[`perf-03-ledger-full-refit-on-the-tap-path`]
`ledger/refit.py:824-836`: when `load_cache` returns None, `_update_incrementally` calls
`refit_user` inline — every observation loaded, `model.fit`, and `np.linalg.inv` over a dense
`(p+n)×(p+n)` matrix (`model.py:799-851`) — on the backend event loop, which is §5.3's "<50 ms"
row. There is a second inline `refit_user` on the non-finite path at `:919-926`. Measured on
synthetic boards: n=300 0.39 s, n=900 1.10 s, n=2000 6.96 s, n=4000 33.4 s. It is hit on the
first tap ever per (user, kind), on every tap after a bundle import, on an `hp_digest` change and
on the NaN fallback. On the drop route the same call is uncaught (finding 8), so a slow fit is a
client timeout that retries a durable write. **It should** stamp `refit_requested_at` — the column
and the 60 s job from `0012`/decision 11 already exist — and return `refit=True, rows=[]`.

**10. `ledger_hyperparams.json` is never validated and is re-read on every request.**
[`ml06-hyperparams-file-unvalidated-breaks-rate-and-rank`, `perf-07-hyperparams-reparsed-on-every-request`]
One repair. `app.py:63-71`'s lifespan sets `artifacts` and `backbone` and never `hyperparams`, so
all three routers' `_hyperparams` fallbacks (`api/rate.py:80-93`, `api/rank.py:98-105`,
`api/tonight.py:105-112`) call `hyperparams.load` per request — a file read plus `from_mapping`
validation plus note generation — and `load_cache` re-digests per tap. `hyperparams.py:219-247`
raises `ValueError` on a bad value and nothing catches it, so one hand-edited or badly restored
constant turns `GET /api/rank` and `POST /api/rate/verdict` into 500s for everyone; the verdict
route reads `hp` as an argument before `_take_card` ever runs. `importer/validate.py` has no
reference to the file at all, so the same `ValueError` escapes `import_bundle`'s transaction as a
traceback rather than a report line. **It should** be loaded once at startup, refused loudly with
a 503 naming the reason, and checked at validation before it is ever swapped in.

### The network call inside the transaction

**11. The Jellyfin push is awaited inside the verdict transaction.**
[`arch-05-rate-session-god-module-and-the-jellyfin-network-call-inside-the-verdict-transaction`,
`syncpush-13-jellyfin-call-inside-the-verdict-transaction`]
One repair, three sites: `rate/session.py:814-838` (verdict), `:880-884` (not-seen) and
`:1022-1027` (correction) all `await _push_state` (`:603-624` → `seen.set_state` → an httpx call
with a 15 s client timeout, `connectors/jellyfin.py:98`) between the observation and `_append`,
inside `async with conn.transaction()`. Measured with a transport that sleeps 1.5 s: the tap took
1.66 s, a second connection could not see the verdict row until 1.67 s, and one backend sat
`idle in transaction` for the whole wait — against a pool of 10 (`db/pool.py:43`). A separate
probe measured a second connection's UPDATE of the same row blocking 0.97 s behind a 1.0 s call.
`sync/seen.py:158-162`'s docstring — "The app-side write is committed before the network call and
never depends on it" — is false for its most frequent caller, and
`ledger/observations.py:560-565`'s `_set_state` says the same thing in the same words: "The
network call belongs to the route, after the commit". `_compensate_push` (`:627-664`) is a second
implementation of `sync/seen.py:100-147` minus its `needs_relink` branch, and it is correctly
placed *outside* its transaction at `:1213-1214` — the precedent is in the same file. **It should**
be after the commit, and the network half should live in `sync/seen.py` where its sibling does.

### The selector and the badge tell the truth

**12. The exploration arm locks onto the board's most-certain pair and calls it the least-compared
title.** [`dd07-exploration-arm-locks-onto-settled-titles`]
`rank/queue.py:180`: `away = [c for c in pool if c.straddle is None] or list(pool)`. On a young
board nothing is in `away`, so it silently draws straddlers. The moment one title leaves the
straddle set, `away` inverts into the handful the model is *most* sure about; the arm picks the
least-compared among them, pairs it with its nearest neighbour, and because the route's
incremental update shrinks exactly those two titles' sigma and never moves the cutpoints, they
stay in `away` forever. Simulated over 500 answers with the route's own semantics: from answer 110
`|away|` was 1–2 of 900, one pair served 78 of 109 exploration draws, and its anchor ended with
the most comparisons on the board (88) while `pair.reason` and the §6.7 line read "the
least-compared title on your board" (`queue.py:197`). Each repeat counts as an independent
Davidson row. §6.3 names the share and nothing else; nothing licenses the straddle filter.
**It should** choose the anchor by fewest `comparisons` over the whole pool and refuse to re-serve
an unordered pair already asked.

**13. `model.straddle` can name a tier two levels away, and always prefers the lower one.**
[`rrh-straddle-names-non-adjacent-tier-and-prefers-downward`]
`ledger/model.py:893-903` sets the reach to `searchsorted(lo)` whenever it differs from the tier,
so a wide posterior crossing two cuts is reported as straddling two tiers down — badge "B/F" on a
five-tier board — and because the lower reach is tested first, a title 0.1 below its upper cut and
0.9 above its lower one is badged with the lower neighbour. `rank/board.py:113-127`'s `straddles`
docstring says "the **adjacent** tier the posterior also reaches", and
`spec_coverage.toml`'s `tonight-rank-straddle-equals-eligible` row says "the one adjacent tier that
exists" — the row already claims the property; no test checks it. `queue._boundary`
(`queue.py:156-160`) consumes the same value, so 70% of draws can be pairs that do not span the
boundary they claim to settle. **It should** restrict the candidates to the two adjacent tiers and,
when both hold, return the nearer cut.

**14. After a drop the straddle chip pairs the drop tier with the model's reach.**
[`cs-18-straddle-badge-pairs-the-drop-tier-with-the-model-reach`]
`rank/board.py:214` renders `rendered = model_tier if assigned_tier is None else assigned_tier`
and `:235-239` builds the chip as `labels[index]/labels[reached]` where `index` is the *rendered*
tier and `reached` comes from the model's own tier. The two halves describe different placements:
s=0.9, σ=1.0, model tier A, dropped into S renders "S/B" — two tiers the posterior does not span,
omitting the one it occupies. The mirror case suppresses the chip entirely when the reach equals
the drop tier while `straddles()` still returns non-None, so a queue-eligible title wears no badge
— breaking the identity §6.3 states in one sentence and the coverage row asserts.
`test_rank_board.py:279` asserts "the badge leads with the rendered tier", enshrining the
deviation. **It should** be built from the posterior's own placement.

**15. `GET /api/rank/queue` re-rolls the arm and the pair on every request.**
[`dd13-rank-queue-arm-and-pair-rerolled-per-get`]
`api/rank.py:296` calls `queue.draw(pool, rng=_rng)` with `_rng = random.SystemRandom()`
(`:63`) on every GET, and `queue.draw` (`queue.py:234-241`) picks the arm before the pair. The seal
carries `(u, k, a, b, arm, n)` and `answer` refuses only on a count mismatch, so a client can
reload until the arm is `uniform_holdout` or until it is not, then answer. The §13 stream is
client-selectable at the arm level — worse than a pair-level door, because the arm's *rate* is the
thing §13 needs independent of everything. **It should** derive the RNG from the queue position,
so arm and pair are a function of what the person has answered rather than of how many times they
reloaded.

**16. The queue tells every member which pairs are the §13 hold-out, ungated.**
[`cs-15-tier-queue-arm-and-reason-ungated`]
`GET /api/rank/queue` (`api/rank.py:284-318`) is the one Rank route that calls neither
`rail.visible_to` nor `rail.redact`. It returns `{**pair.public(), ...}` and `Pair.public()`
(`queue.py:88-96`) ships both `arm` and `reason`; the hold-out reason at `queue.py:221` reads
"uniform-random, held out — this pair never tunes the model" and
`rank/+page.svelte:304` renders `{rank.pair.reason}` unconditionally. The route's own docstring at
`:290-293` says "the arm travels sealed rather than plain". It does not. A person told a
comparison does not count has been given a reason to answer it carelessly, and those are the 10%
§13 calls the only admissible evaluation data. **It should** go through the gate, with an
arm-independent user-facing line.

**17. Every "into a tier" drop invents a duel nobody made.**
[`cs-03-tier-drop-fabricates-a-neighbour-duel`]
§6.3 gives an into-a-tier drop a bare `tier_edit` and reserves the two margin-less duels for a drop
*between two titles*. `frontend/src/lib/rank.svelte.js:227-236` returns
`{above: <last entry>, below: null}` whenever `beforeTitleId` is null — which is both the phone tap
(`dropLifted`, `+page.svelte:212`) and a pointer drop on the row's empty space (`:223`) — and
`rank/drop.py:107-112` writes `duel(title_a=above, title_b=title_id, outcome='A')`. On phones, the
primary form factor, tap-to-tier is the only way to move a title, so every promotion into a
non-empty tier writes an append-only comparison the person never made, always in the same
direction. The two coverage tests call `drop.drop()` with no neighbours — a body the shipped
client never sends for a non-empty tier. **It should** send `{above: null, below: null}` and leave
`drop.py` alone, which legitimately accepts one neighbour for a genuine end-of-tier insert.

**18. Under a filter, a drop's "new neighbours" are the visible ones, and the server checks
nothing.** [`cs-60-drop-neighbours-read-from-the-filtered-board`]
The client computes neighbours from `rank.tiers`, which is the board as filtered
(`rank.svelte.js:228`), and the drop route deliberately answers with the filtered board
(`api/rank.py:245-252`). `rank/drop.py:88-98` refuses only self-reference and `above == below`: it
never checks that either id is *in* the tier being dropped into, so a stale board — two tabs, or
one read before a nightly refit — posts neighbours that have moved and the claim is stored anyway.
**It should** resolve each named neighbour's current tier before writing and refuse when it does
not match; the filtered case then needs a stated answer rather than an accidental one.

### Copy the app cannot support

**19. The age why-line says "40 years" for every film older than forty.**
[`rrh-age-why-line-saturates-at-forty-years`]
`rate/queue.py:326` derives the printed number from the *clipped* feature —
`years_out = round(age * AGE_SATURATION_YEARS)` with `age` clipped to 1.0 by the SQL at `:265-268`
— so any title released before 1986 prints exactly 40. 6,806 of the 19,071 corpus titles are older
than that, and the age term is the dominant one — the one the line names — whenever the title is
unowned with no playback, co-seen or crowd signal. §6.8 makes that register normative.
**It should** compute the printed years from the year, with the SQL's own reference year selected
so Python and Postgres share a clock; `age` stays clipped for the logistic.

**20. With no battle pool the surface says "You've rated everything we can queue right now".**
[`rrh-drained-copy-false-in-battle-mode`]
`DRAINED` (`rate/session.py:66-71`) is the only empty-state copy and `:1274` serves it whenever
`card` is None — including Battle mode with fewer than two same-class verdicted titles, where the
queue is not drained at all. A first-week member who taps Battle is told they have rated
everything. **It should** choose the copy by cause.

**21. Correction fallbacks and the banner head-redraw serve a sweep card in a battle slot with no
marker.** [`rrh-serving-says-battle-card-is-an-unmarked-sweep`]
`_redraw_pair`'s two fallbacks and `ensure_card`'s head redraw (`rate/session.py:390`) stash a
sweep card without the `substituted_for` the ordinary substitution path sets (`:400`, `:408`),
while `:1270` computes `serving` from the slot rather than the card. The frontend masks the
counter (`rate/+page.svelte:62-66`) but the explanation tied to `substituted_for` is lost and the
API contract is inconsistent. **It should** mark the substitution wherever the type flips.

**22. Rate's copy denies that Tonight selects.** [`cs-61-rate-copy-denies-that-tonight-selects`]
`PAIR_SELECTION_COPY` (`frontend/src/lib/rate.svelte.js:49-51`) ships the pre-decision sentence —
"the clever ones only pay off in the tier queue" — rendered at `RateRail.svelte:57` and mirrored in
`rate/battle.py:5` and `:143`. 54a's replacement, which names both the tier queue and tonight's
round, appears nowhere in the repository, while `tonight/round.py`'s selector is adaptive. The
behaviour is right; the explanation is false one surface over, and §6.8 makes every claim the app
states about its own model a matter of honesty. **It should** carry 54a's sentence, in all three
places, ASCII.

**23. The pending-verdicts banner names titles the session cannot serve, and its `mode=sweep` is
dead on both ends.** [`rrh-banner-names-a-title-the-session-cannot-serve`]
`home/shelves.py:233-243` builds the population with no kind predicate and `:260` appends
`mode=sweep` to a CTA that `api/rate.py:130-138` does not declare (`current` takes `head` only) and
`rate/+page.svelte:75,99` does not read. A films-only session with a seen-but-unrated series gets
a banner naming the series and a CTA that lands on an unrelated film — proposal 150's own failure
mode, "a prompt that names titles and then presents a different one is worse than no prompt".
**It should** either filter the population by the live session's kinds or make the CTA
self-sufficient; either way `mode=sweep` stops being a lie.

**24. Mix serves two sweeps at the block boundary.** [`cs-64-mix-serves-two-sweeps-at-the-block-boundary`]
`card_type_for` (`rate/session.py:136-146`) returns sweep on odd slots and `advance` (`:150-158`)
rolls slot 15 to slot 1. Fifteen is odd, so every block ends and the next begins with a sweep —
one consecutive-same pair per block, eight sweeps to seven battles. Over §6.1's 50–100-verdict
