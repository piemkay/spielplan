> **Decision 168 — Does the K-nearest-straddler bound ship with the vectorised selector rewrite?**  
> Ship only the vectorised rewrite. It reproduces round.expected_straddlers bit-for-bit (max |diff| = 0.0 over 12 pools x 200 pairs) and brings the worst realistic board — a guest of a fresh household, 574-591 straddlers of a 696/716-title pool — to 295-333 ms against §6's 1.5 s, so the K-bound buys headroom nobody needs. The K-bound is a rule change: at the real measured priors K=24 already picks a different pair on 2 of 36 member boards, and at a tighter score spread (sd 0.25) K=24 diverges on 25% of fresh boards and costs up to +0.94 expected unresolved candidates. Its safety is a property of the score distribution, which moves as members accumulate verdicts and nothing guards. If the timing guard later fails on the reference box, reach for perf-01's third mitigation (persist the participant's beliefs so a GET does not replay), not for the K-bound — that one is orthogonal to 54c's sentence.
>
> **Decision 169 — Does a host-only end-room control ship in the initial release?**  
> Yes — POST /api/tonight/sessions/{id}/end, host only, writing STATE_ABANDONED through rooms.set_state, in the same diff as play.settle. Option (b) must be rejected on a factual error, not on taste: its premise that 'a member who walks away leaves the room open until the next open_session abandons it' is false. open_session's abandon is scoped `WHERE host_user_id = $2 AND state = 'open'`, and backend/tests/test_tonight_integration.py::test_a_started_room_is_not_abandoned_by_a_second_one asserts on purpose that a `voting` room survives it. STATE_ABANDONED has exactly one writer in the whole backend, and no worker job touches `session` at all — so today a room that reaches `voting` and loses a seat is live for ever: on §6.2 step 2's list on every household device, holding its room code against the `session_room_code_live` unique index, and settle cannot help because everyone_finished is false. The end control is the only remedy that works while the household is still in the room.
>
> **Decision 173 — Where does the axis artifact live, and does 54d's split surfacing ship at all?**  
> Ship without axes, and say so in the spec: on release data 54d cannot fire, and pretending otherwise is the defect. I am overturning the recommendation's first branch — re-running the corpus exporter cannot produce the artifact. mdc/export_bundle.py::_export_vocab is a blind copy of the regular files in data/dna_vocab/v1/, and upstream that directory holds axes for one facet of eleven, in a non-conforming shape (mdc/dna/vocab.py:334 — "Pacing only, so far"). Option (a) is not an export run; it is an upstream authoring project of 582 term weights and 11 pole pairs, of which 19 terms (3%) exist today. Do not gate the release on it. Reject (b) as written too: reading vocab_pacing_axes_v1.tsv buys one facet, five unnamed sub-axes and no poles, and would put a raw facet id in user-facing copy ("You're split on pacing_energy"). Take the correction half of (b) instead — move the loader off the `axes/` subdirectory it invented, because the exporter skips subdirectories by construction and an artifact placed there could never reach a bundle.

## 1. Why these changes are one milestone

The review's coupling map calls this the sharpest file-locality case in the whole project, and
names splitting it an anti-seam. Three files carry almost all of it: `backend/spielplan/api/tonight.py`
(706 lines, the god-router), `backend/spielplan/tonight/play.py` (640 lines, whose three functions
`start` / `record_answer` / `state_for` are rewritten from six independent directions), and
`frontend/src/lib/tonight.svelte.js` (500 lines, whose `refresh()` and `loadRound()` are rewritten
four ways). Handing correctness to one agent and performance to another hands them `round.py`,
`play.py` and `rooms.py` at the same time.

The four blockers are one ordered chain, not four parallel tasks:

1. **Make the selector cheap before threading it.** `dd15`'s own mitigation says so in as many
   words — threading a 36 s job only moves the stall — and every assertion after this point has to
   be exercised at real pool size rather than on the four-to-six-title fixtures, or it is measuring
   the fixture again. This is the failure M4.5's whole close-out was about.
2. **Then give the lifecycle one home.** `arch-06` moves the `voting → ballot` transition out of
   `_announce` into `play.settle`, the reveal-card assembly into `tonight/result.slate`, and the
   push fan-out into `rooms.invite`. Settle-from-a-read is the single seam through which four
   separate permanent-stall defects heal: the two-or-three-candidate round that converges at zero
   answers, the seat orphaned by the join/start race, the room whose combine failed once, and the
   evening whose last seat walked away. Without it each of those needs its own cure.
3. **Then the join claim.** `tq2-join-start` needs `play.start` to claim the room before it builds
   the pool; its second half — a seat that can never be asked anything ends itself — routes through
   the settle path from step 2 and cannot be written before it.
4. **Then the guest hand-off.** The backend already permits a host to write a guest's ballot
   (`api/tonight.py:165-173`); the missing half is one `activeSeat` in the store. That store field
   is the same field `fe-10`'s refresh clobber needs and the same one `dd-fe-tonight-reload` reads.
   Freezing a backend flag and a frontend `activeSeat` across a milestone boundary would ship half
   a contract.

Decision 165's TV retirement rides here because it is a deletion inside these same files:
`frontend/src/lib/tonight.svelte.js:54-55`'s `JOIN_CAPTION` advertises the TV route to every lobby,
`e2e/specs/05-milestones.spec.js:64-76` names the TV spec in its comment, and
`spec_coverage.toml:1675`'s `tonight-rank-tv-kiosk-route` names five tests in a file this milestone
deletes — `test_spec_coverage.py`'s rule 2 fails the build the moment those functions stop existing.
That is not an independent chore; it is a red gate inside this diff.

The remainder — the hold-out seal, the combine repairs, the socket, solo, the copy and the
instrument — are all in the same six files, and every one of them is a rule the exit criterion's
"a real evening resolves" sentence has to be true through.

## 2. What is broken today

Forty-seven findings, grouped where two are one repair. Line numbers are at branch `m45`
head `50753a3` plus the uncommitted spec amendments, and every one was re-read against the tree
while writing this.

### A. The selector is unaffordable, and pays for itself twice per tap

**1. The pair search is superlinear and uncapped.** `[perf-01-tonight-selector-superlinear-on-request-path]`
`round.expected_straddlers` (`backend/spielplan/tonight/round.py:405-422`) evaluates every one of
the four possible answers by calling `update()` — a dict copy of all *n* beliefs
(`round.py:288-315`) — and then `straddlers()`, an O(n) rescan with its own sort (`round.py:227`).
`select()` (`round.py:447-533`) calls that for every unordered pair of the straddling set.
`pool.build` caps nothing (`pool.py:255-277` returns every owned title of the kind every seated
member has scored). The real bundle yields **696 candidates** at the default 130-minute budget;
`round.initial`'s `prior_var = 1.0` (`round.py:168`) against a measured owned-pool `user_score`
sd of 0.50 leaves **344 of 696 straddling**, so the search covers ~59k pairs: **36.2 s per replay**
measured on the real priors, 23.9 s after one answer. A guest seat — `GUEST_VAR_FACTOR = 4.0`
(`round.py:101`) — has 660 straddlers and costs **125.9 s**. A GET of the round runs one replay,
a POST runs two, and `finish` runs one per seat. §6 preamble (`docs/spielplan-spec_v2.1.md:211`)
budgets **1.5 s per battle**.
*It should* return the identical pair 54c's rule names, in well under half a second at 700
candidates. The expected straddler count after an answer is computable from the two moved beliefs
plus a `searchsorted` against the moved cut — the whole rescan and the dict copy are avoidable
without touching the rule.

**2. That search runs on the request event loop, inside the answer transaction.**
`[dd15-selector-runs-on-the-request-event-loop]` `grep -rn 'to_thread\|run_in_executor'` over
`backend/spielplan/tonight` and `api/tonight.py` returns nothing. `play.record_answer` opens its
transaction at `play.py:322` and replays at `play.py:355` — **inside** it — so the participant row
lock and the open transaction last as long as the search. One phone's answer therefore stalls the
other phone's GET, the WebSocket hub and `/api/health` on a 4 vCPU box.
*It should* run the pure replay under `asyncio.to_thread` (it touches no DB) and outside the
answer transaction, **after** repair 1 lands, not instead of it.

**3. Every tap runs the round twice and parses the pool snapshot three times.**
`[perf-12-tonight-answer-route-does-double-work-and-reparses-the-pool-snapshot]`
`api/tonight.py:391` calls `play.record_answer` (which reads `snapshot_of` and replays), then
`:410` calls `_announce` (which reads progress), then `:411` calls `play.state_for` (which reads
`snapshot_of` at `play.py:268` and replays again at `:270`). `_as_snapshot` (`play.py:98-110`)
rebuilds the candidates / scores / dna / axes dicts from the jsonb every time, and `play.start`
stores a DNA vector for every candidate (`play.py:160-177`), so on a 300-title pool that is on the
order of a megabyte of JSON decoded per read. Undo and escape have the same shape.
*It should* be one snapshot read and one replay per tap: `record_answer` already computed
`played` and can return it.

### B. The session has no lifecycle owner, so anything that stops progressing stops for ever

**4. The `voting → ballot` transition exists only inside a POST handler.**
`[arch-06-tonight-lifecycle-and-reveal-assembly-live-in-the-router]` `_announce`
(`api/tonight.py:461-477`) is the only caller of `play.finish`, and it runs from the answer, undo
and escape handlers only (`:410`, `:436`, `:456`) — after the answer has already committed. If
`finish` raises once (a dropped connection, a container restart mid-request), the answer stands,
the request 500s, and the room sits in `voting` with `ended_by` set on every seat. No read path
retries: the lobby (`:297-315`) reports `voting`, `GET /ballot` (`:483-499`) answers 200 with an
empty slate, `GET /result` (`:526-538`) answers 409 `still_voting`, and every write is refused.
The same router also assembles the reveal card — `def card(row)` at `:554` with an import inside
the closure, the wildcard label, `fit_line(budget or 130)` and the runners-up sort — and does the
push fan-out, so neither rule is testable without the ASGI app.
*It should* have `play.settle(conn, session_id, *, z) -> bool` holding the body of `:470-477`
minus the hub frames, called by `GET /sessions/{id}`, `GET /sessions/{id}/ballot` and
`GET /sessions/{id}/result` before they read. `finish` already runs under
`pg_advisory_xact_lock` with DELETE/INSERT (`play.py:611`), so the repeat is safe by construction,
and `ballot.resolve` is idempotent at the other end.

**5. A member who joins while the host starts is seated into a pool that has no scores for them,
and the room then deadlocks.** `[tq2-join-start-race-seats-an-orphan-into-a-started-room]`
Two halves of one hole. `rooms.join` reads the session row **once, outside** its retry loop
(`rooms.py:204-206`) and consults that stale `row['state']` inside it (`:226`); its INSERT carries
no state predicate. `play.start` is not a transaction at all — it reads the seats (`play.py:137`),
builds the pool (`:138-145`), and only then flips `state` (`:174-181`). So a join is admitted for
the entire duration of the build, which on a real library is tens of seconds. That seat has no
entry in the frozen `scores` map, so `pool_scores_for` returns `{}`, `round.boundary` returns None
(`round.py:217`), `stop_reason` is `converged` at zero answers, and no pair is ever served.
`_end` (`play.py:361`) is reachable only from `record_answer` (needs a pair) and `escape` (refused
below five answers), so `ended_by` stays NULL, `everyone_finished` (`play.py:464-468`) stays false,
and nothing can end the room. Reproduced twice independently, including through the real HTTP
routes with the pool build slowed to 0.6 s.
*It should* claim the room in `start`'s first statement and make join's rule a property of its own
statement, and a seat that can never be asked anything must end itself rather than strand.

**6. A round over a pool of two or three candidates converges at zero answers and never ends.**
`[dd12-group-round-on-two-or-three-candidates-never-ends]` `round.boundary` returns None when
`len(beliefs) <= SHORTLIST_SIZE` (`round.py:217`), so `straddlers()` is empty and `stop_reason`
is `CONVERGED` before any answer (`round.py:344-345`). `play.start` admits any pool with ≥ 2
candidates (`play.py:146-152`). `state_for` then returns `pair=None` with `ended_by=None`
(`play.py:275-291`), and the same dead end as defect 5 follows. Reproduced end-to-end: start 200,
round `{stop_reason: converged, pair: None}`, lobby `voting`, escape 409 `too_early`, undo 409,
ballot 200 with an empty slate. A four-title pool ends normally by cap. This is a "series" night
with three owned shows, or a 60-minute budget on a modest library — no race needed.
*It should* either refuse admission below four candidates with a reason that says so (54d fixes the
slate at three finalists plus a wildcard, which is four) or go straight to the ballot — an owner
call — and either way the belt-and-braces settle from defect 5 must catch it.

**7. Nothing can end a started room.** `[tf-no-route-ends-a-started-room]`
`rooms.open_session`'s abandon clause takes rooms in state `open` only (`rooms.py:176-180`,
`state = $3` with `$3 = STATE_OPEN`). There is no end / leave / close route, and `worker.py:257`'s
job registry has no Tonight job at all. A started room whose seats never all finish is live for
ever: on every household device's open-rooms list (`api/tonight.py:218-221` lists every session
with `ended_at IS NULL`), holding the member's seat so bootstrap restores every device into it,
and unfinishable. Recovery is SQL against `session`.
*It should* have a host-only `POST /api/tonight/sessions/{id}/end` calling a new
`rooms.end_session` that sets `STATE_ABANDONED` — 0013's `session_ended_states` CHECK already ties
`ended_at` to that state, so **no migration**. §14 risk 6 wants the votes kept, so it is not a
DELETE.

**8. A member seated in a room the host abandons keeps a lobby, and the API still serves that
seat.** `[tonight-abandoned-room-still-serves-its-seats]` `play._participant` (`play.py:188-199`)
selects `s.state` but not `s.ended_at`, and `_seat_for` (`api/tonight.py:151-175`) selects neither,
so `GET /seats/{id}/round` on an abandoned room answers 200 with `stop_reason: converged` and no
pair instead of 404. On the client, `refresh()` (`tonight.svelte.js:183-196`) has branches for
revealed / `ballot` / `voting` only, so the member's phone shows a lobby for a room that no longer
exists.
*It should* raise `RoundError('no_room')` — `_room_error` already maps `no_room` to 404
(`api/tonight.py:136`) — and the client should leave with a line saying the host closed the room.

**9. `escape_available` stays true after the round has ended.**
`[tq2-escape-available-true-on-a-finished-round]` `play.state_for:280` computes it from
`answered_count` alone and never consults `ended_by`, under a docstring (`play.py:262-265`) that
says the availability travels precisely so the client does not decide for itself. `play.escape`
(`play.py:418-432`) then refuses the call with `round_over`.
*It should* be `row["ended_by"] is None and round_rules.escape_available(row["answered_count"])`
— one line — keeping `round.escape_available` as the pure predicate about the count.

### C. Every write guard is a check-then-act on an autocommit connection

**10. Two simultaneous answers under one card token: the second is a 500, not a 409.**
`[dd08-tonight-answer-race-answers-500-instead-of-409]` `record_answer` reads the participant with
a plain SELECT and checks `seq != row['answered_count'] + 1` at `play.py:316-319` — **outside** the
transaction that opens at `:322`. Both racers pass, and the loser's INSERT collides with 0014's
partial unique index `session_answer_seq`. Reproduced 5 races of 6. The client only re-reads on a
409 (`tonight.svelte.js:273`), so on the 500 it shows an error over a dead card. 0014's own comment
claims the index makes "a double-submit at one seq impossible even when two taps race the counter"
— true for rows, false for the HTTP contract.
*It should* move the `ended_by`, `_turn_is_open` and seq checks inside the transaction with a
locked read (`FOR UPDATE OF p` — the JOIN to `session` means `OF p`, not a bare `FOR UPDATE`), so
the loser waits, re-reads and raises `stale_pair` → 409 through the existing mapping.

**11. An undo racing an answer wedges the seat for ever.**
`[tf-undo-racing-an-answer-wedges-the-seat-forever]` `retract` tombstones the last live row
(`play.py:392-394`), re-reads `_answers` (`:395`) and writes `answered_count = len(answers)`
(`:412`) — the rows that survive its own tombstone. If an answer commits in between, the recount
includes the new row: live seqs `{1,2,4}` with `answered_count = 3`. The next card is minted at
seq 4 again, the INSERT collides, and **every subsequent tap answers 500 for ever** — exactly the
dead end 0014's comment says it removed. Reproduced.
*It should* mint the seq from the rows, `SELECT coalesce(max(seq),0)+1 FROM session_answer WHERE
participant_id = $1` (tombstones included, so a seq is never reused), keep `answered_count` as
displayed progress only, and take the same row lock as defect 10 so the two writers serialise.

**12. Two concurrent ballot submits from one participant collide on the unique index.**
`[schema-ballot-double-submit-hits-unique-index-500]` `ballot.submit` does DELETE-then-INSERT
inside a transaction with no lock (`ballot.py:87-97`); the second tab's DELETE sees no committed
rows and its INSERTs fail on `session_ballot_one_per_title` (`0013_tonight.sql:261-262`). Probe:
`['UniqueViolationError', 'dict']` with 4 rows and correct state. `app.py:114-117` turns every
`asyncpg.PostgresError` into a 500, so the losing tab shows a database error for a vote that was
in fact recorded — on the one moment 54e makes social.
*It should* take `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on (a ballot constant, participant_id) at the top of the
transaction — the idiom `play.finish:611` already uses — or replace DELETE+INSERT with
`ON CONFLICT ... DO UPDATE` plus a DELETE of rows no longer on the slate.

### D. The frontend models one seat per device, so pass-the-phone has no representation

**13. A room opened with any guest can never reach the reveal.**
`[tq2-guest-room-can-never-be-revealed]` `ballot.submitted_count` counts every row in
`session_participant`, guests included (`ballot.py:100-110`), and `tally` refuses with
`still_voting` until submitted ≥ seated (`ballot.py:117-125`). The round has a hand-the-phone
control — `+page.svelte:353-359` renders `pass to {guest.name}` and calls
`loadRound(guest.participant_id)` — but the ballot Submit thirty lines later is bound to
`submitBallot(me?.participant_id)` (`+page.svelte:384`) with no equivalent, and a guest seat
carries `user_id NULL` so it is never `me`. The count stops one short for ever. **The backend is
already complete**: `_seat_for` (`api/tonight.py:165-173`) deliberately lets a host write to a
guest seat in a session they host. Proven in the review by integration test: host submits → 200
`submitted=1 of 2`, result 409; host submits the *guest's* ballot → 200 `2 of 2`, result 200 with
a winner.
*It should* hold the seat this device is voting for in the store, render a `pass to {guest.name}`
control in the ballot block mirroring `:353-359`, call `submitBallot(ballotSeat)`, and clear
`tonight.approved` on every hand-off exactly as `loadRound` clears `tonight.rail`
(`tonight.svelte.js:236-244`) — or the incoming guest sees the previous person's selections and
54e's blindness is broken by the fix itself.

**14. During a guest's turn, any household frame or reconnect replaces the guest's round with the
host's seat.** `[fe-10-tonight-guest-handoff-clobbered-by-refresh]` `refresh()` unconditionally
re-reads `seen.me.participant_id` while voting (`tonight.svelte.js:192`), and it is invoked from
the `rooms.changed` handler (broadcast household-wide on every open, start and reveal —
`api/tonight.py:241, 333, 517`), from `lobby` and `reveal` frames (`:439`), and after every socket
reconnect (`:446-453`), which a screen lock is enough to cause. Nothing records which seat this
device is playing. `escape()` (`:297-310`) makes it deterministic rather than a race: a guest
tapping "just pick for us" ends with `await refresh()` and lands on the host's seat.
*It should* hold `tonight.activeSeat` set by `loadRound()` and cleared by `leave()`, have
`refresh()` re-read that seat (falling back to `me` when nothing is displayed or the displayed
seat has ended), and have `escape()` re-read the seat it just ended. The `rooms.changed` refresh
must **not** be dropped — the comment at `:431-436` documents why it is there.

**15. Reloading while a room is open but not started lands on the door.**
`[dd-fe-tonight-reload-into-an-open-room-lands-on-the-door]` `bootstrap()` (`:119-134`) finds the
room, sets `tonight.lobby` and calls `refresh()`, which sets `step` only for revealed / `ballot` /
`voting`. There is no branch for `state === 'open'`, so `step` stays `'door'` while the device
holds a lobby — the state the restore was written to prevent.
*It should* add `else if (seen.state === 'open') tonight.step = 'lobby';`.

### E. The socket holds the pool, the hub holds the request, and the client storms

**16. The WebSocket route holds an asyncpg connection across its sends.**
`[tf-websocket-holds-pool-connections]` `channel()` opens `async with db_pool.acquire()` at
`api/tonight.py:682` and awaits `socket.send_json` **twice inside it** (`:683-695`). A client that
is connected but not reading blocks that send indefinitely — there is no timeout on this path,
unlike the hub's guard — and the pool connection is pinned. `db/pool.py:39` sets `max_size=10`.
Reproduced with ten hanging sockets: pool size 10 / idle 0, and an ordinary `GET /api/auth/me`
then timed out. This is not a Tonight-only failure; Rate, Home and auth stop answering.
(Status in the review: *contested* on severity, but the reading is unambiguous and was re-verified
against the tree while writing this.)
*It should* build both payloads inside the acquired connection, exit the block, then send — and
wrap each send in `asyncio.wait_for(..., channel_rules.SEND_TIMEOUT)`, closing the socket on
timeout. Raising `max_size` is not the fix; it moves the wall.

**17. One suspended phone adds 5 s to every Tonight write, and is never told to reconnect.**
`[tf-stalled-subscriber-adds-five-seconds-to-every-write]` `_announce`, `open_session`, `start`
and `submit_ballot` all await the hub fan-out inside the request (`api/tonight.py:466-467`, `:232`,
`:332-333`, `:516-519`). `Hub._deliver` gives each socket `SEND_TIMEOUT = 5.0`
(`channel.py:46`, delivery at `:125-134`), so a connected-but-not-draining device adds five seconds
to the answering phone's request against §6's 1.5 s. On timeout the hub calls `self.unsubscribe(sub)`
and **nothing else** — it never closes the socket — and the client's only re-read path is
`socket.onclose` (`tonight.svelte.js:446`), so that device is silently deaf for the rest of the
evening.
*It should* add `close` to the `Socket` Protocol (`channel.py:74-79`) and call it in the except
branch, and take the fan-out off the request path with `asyncio.create_task` — the module docstring
already says frames are at-most-once nudges to re-read, and nothing in the response depends on
delivery.

**18. The socket reconnects every 1.5 s for ever with a re-read per attempt.**
`[fe-lc-07-ws-reconnect-storm]` `socket.onclose` (`tonight.svelte.js:446-453`) schedules a fixed
1.5 s retry with no backoff, no jitter and no cap, and each attempt re-opens the socket and then
fires `refresh()` and `loadRooms()`. Two independent measurements: 60 simulated seconds → 40
sockets and 117 HTTP calls; a 9 s downtime → 7 attempts and 6 reads. §10's swap sequence ends in
exactly the backend restart this hammers.
*It should* use exponential backoff with jitter capped near 30 s, reset in a new `socket.onopen`,
with the re-read moved into `onopen` so it happens once per successful reconnect.

**19. Leaving `/tonight` while its bootstrap is in flight leaks a socket for ever.**
`[fe-lc-08-orphaned-tonight-socket-per-fast-navigation]` `+page.svelte:72-80`:
`onMount(async () => { const resumed = await bootstrap(); if (watching === null) watch(resumed) })`
with `onDestroy(() => disconnect())`. If the page is destroyed mid-bootstrap, `onDestroy` runs
first (while `disconnect` is still the no-op default at `:57`), then the continuation calls
`watch()`, which opens a socket and assigns the new `disconnect` into a closure nobody will call.
Every such navigation leaks a live channel that keeps mutating `tonight.rooms/lobby/step` from a
page that no longer exists.

## 7. What this milestone deliberately does NOT do

- **It does not ship the DNA axes.** `[dd12-no-axis-artifact]` `[dd12-real-bundle-ships-no-axes]`
  The corpus exports none, and part (1) of that mitigation is corpus-side work (proposal 140) in a
  different repository. So every repair in step 10 lands on a branch that **cannot execute on the
  shipped bundle**: `contested` is None on every real night, `session_result.conflict` is always
  NULL, and 54c's widest-axis tie-break is always 0.0. They are verified at the pure layer with
  hand-seeded axes, and the milestone's contribution to the gap is *honesty* — the warn, the admin
  report line, the README/TESTING sentence. Do not read a green split test as a statement about
  release behaviour.
- **It does not re-tune the round.** `[cs-08]` `[dd15-prior-var]` The measured table goes in front
  of the owner beside `M4-open-points §1.1` (which this milestone's evidence **refutes**: narrowing

- **It does not re-calibrate the split trigger.** `[tq2-split-trigger]` Owner question O4. The

  edited to match the code.
- **It does not persist the participant's beliefs.** `perf-01`'s option 3 would make a GET replay
  nothing at all, at the cost of a migration and a new consistency question. The vectorised search

- **It does not bound the pair search to K straddlers.** That is a change to which pair 54c's rule

- **It does not add a worker sweep for idle rooms.** M4-plan resolution 10's 6 h expiry job belongs

- **It does not build user management.** Two real accounts are **M4.6**'s deliverable and this

## 8. Risks and gotchas

**The chain is an ordering constraint, not a preference.** Threading before vectorising moves a
36 s stall onto a thread and calls it fixed. Writing the seat-ends-itself repair before
`play.settle` gives it nothing to settle into. Shipping the two-candidate admission change before
`combine.py:315`'s missing `next()` default turns a silent deadlock into a 500 on the last answer of
an evening.

**`play.start`'s claim has a rollback obligation.** The claim (`state = 'voting'`) is taken before
the pool is built, and `play.py:146-152` still raises `empty_pool` after it. A refusal that leaves
the room claimed in `voting` with no snapshot recreates the exact defect step 3 closes. Put the
claim and the raise in one transaction, or release on the raise, and test it.

**`is_holdout` has two callers, one of which has no participant.** `api/tonight.py:641-642`
re-derives the arm for solo from `seq` alone. A rate keyed on anything not stable per caller
reclassifies a stored solo answer between two requests, and the stored group `selection` comes from
the sealed pair at serve time, so group rows are safe but solo rows are not. Key the group case on
the seat id and the solo case on `user.id`.

**Never edit an applied migration.** `0013_tonight.sql` and `0014_tonight_undo.sql` are
sha256-checksummed and a mismatch is a hard startup error. Several repairs here are *about* those
files' invariants (the partial unique index, the `session_ended_states` CHECK, `session_result_rank`)
and every one of them is satisfied without touching them.

**Deleting a named test breaks the build, by design.** `test_spec_coverage.py` rule 2 fails when a
row names a test that no longer exists, so `e2e/specs/16-tonight-tv.spec.js` and
`spec_coverage.toml:1675` must go in the **same commit**. Likewise
`e2e/specs/05-milestones.spec.js` "fails by design when a surface ships": the guest-reveal work
changes what that file's Tonight test should assert.

**`MILESTONES` order is authored.** `M4.12` goes after `M4.10` in the list. String sorting would put
`M4.10` before `M4.5`; `_at_or_before` uses the list index, so the position in the literal is the
contract.

**The e2e suite is stateful, filename-ordered, one worker, and the `phone` project (iPhone 13,
WebKit) is primary.** Extend `14-tonight.spec.js`; do not add a file. Do not parallelise. And
`npm --prefix e2e run fresh` is the canonical run — a plain `playwright test` against a used stack
makes first-boot and bundle specs skip and fake-pass.

**Integration tests skip silently without `TEST_DATABASE_URL`.** A green no-DB run has not exercised
a single one of the eight integration rows above. Bring Postgres up with
`docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f ops/compose.dev.yml up -d db` (overlays are never
auto-loaded).

**`ops/devstub.py` is a harness, not the app.** Several Tonight shapes exist there too;
`backend/spielplan/api/` wins on any disagreement, and a devstub edit is never a fix.

**Console and test output must stay ASCII.** Windows cp1252 consoles crash on decorative glyphs, and
`ops/m412_exit_criterion.py` is run on exactly such a console.

**Diffs stay surgical.** Line length 108 is deliberate because spec sentences are quoted inline;
`ruff format` is not enforced, so do not reformat `round.py` or `play.py` while working in them.
Comments argue why and cite the clause — `§N.M`, `decision N`, or `54a`–`54h` — and work that cannot
cite its clause reads as off-convention.

**Two findings cite coverage row ids that do not exist** — `tonight-rank-split-reserved-slot` and
`tonight-solo-three-picks`. The real ids are `tonight-rank-split-reserves-third-slot` (`:1270`) and
`tonight-rank-solo-lands-on-picks` (`:1354`). Register against those.

**Do not close an owner question by choosing.** Six of them are listed in step 14; three block work
in steps 3, 10 and 11. A silent choice on O3 changes §6.2 step 3's arithmetic, and a silent choice
on O4 makes §6.2's normative ~14.5% unreadable as a check.
