# The road to M5 — eleven milestones, and the plan that groups them

This is the handover document for the pre-release work. A full review of the `m45` branch produced 439 distinct
findings; this plan puts 389 of them into eleven milestones that all land **before M5**, and names the other 50 as
deferred with a reason. Each milestone has its own document in this directory (`M4.6-plan.md` … `M4.16-plan.md`),
written to be handed to one implementation agent with no other context.

**How the grouping was chosen.** Three independent partitionings of the same findings were produced — by subsystem
and file locality, by root cause and invariant, and by household-visible promise — and judged against a coupling map
of which files many findings touch and which fixes must precede which. The file-locality partitioning won, because
it is the only one under which two milestones never own the same hotspot file; the invariant partitioning supplied
the language each milestone uses to say what it closes, and the household partitioning supplied the ordering and the
register of the exit criteria. Every criterion below is a sentence a person can fail on a real install with the real
corpus bundle, in the voice of §12's existing ones.

### The rules, before you write a line

1. **The decisions below are binding.** Seven milestone plans were drafted while some questions were
   open and present two branches; each of those carries a preamble at the top naming the decision
   that settled it. Where a plan offers a choice a decision has made, follow the decision.
2. **Take your migration number from the ledger in this file, not from your plan.** Several plans
   independently say "0016 is the next free number". It is free exactly once.
3. **`spec_coverage.toml` carries one global `current_milestone`.** Only the milestone holding it
   has a red gate; every other in-flight milestone appends its rows without raising the scalar.
   Adding `"M4.6"` to `test_spec_coverage.py`'s hard-coded `MILESTONES` list and adding at least one
   M4.6 row must be **one commit** inside M4.6, or the build is red for everyone.
4. **Two diffs fail the build by design** and are budgeted inside the milestone that trips them:
   `05-milestones.spec.js`'s "arrives with M5" assertion dies inside M4.6 with the Users tab, and
   the coverage gate's named-test check fires inside M4.12 when the TV spec and its row are deleted.
   Neither is a flake, and neither may be silenced with a waiver.
5. **`CLAUDE.md` still governs.** Surgical diffs, cite the clause, never edit an applied migration,
   no speculative abstractions, ASCII in console output.

### What to do first

The review's own recommendation, which the owner has read: **do not work the eleven milestones in
order from a cold start.** The largest remaining unknown is not another finding — it is that nobody
has run this app against the real corpus bundle with two real people. §12 made M2 the gate ("50–100
verdicts each produce visibly personal rankings — the first real-user validation of whether the
corpus transfers"), and that gate has never been run. Every number in the repo before this review
came from an eight-title fixture, which is exactly why the review found a title card that throws on
838 of 839 owned titles, a fifth of the library served at `e(t) = 0`, and a Tonight pair that takes
36 seconds.

So the suggested first week is four fixes drawn from three different milestones, enough to make the
app usable on real data — not shippable, usable:

| Fix | From | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| The title card's duplicate credit keys | M4.9 | 838 of 839 owned titles throw; hours, client-side |
| `cs-01`, the zeroed Backbone coordinates | M4.13 | 20.6% of the library cannot be personalised at all, so it corrupts the very thing you would be evaluating |
| The Tonight selector, vectorised | M4.12 | 36 s per pair is not a slow evening, it is an unusable one |
| Accounts | — | Use `curl` against `POST /api/setup/members`; do **not** build the Users screen yet |

Then import the real bundle, have both members rate 50–100 titles, and run one Friday night. Then
re-prioritise from what that felt like: some of the 439 will feel urgent and many will feel like a
reviewer being thorough. After that, work the milestones in the order below — that ordering is
correct for *shipping*, which is a different question from *is this good*.

If you are being asked to work a milestone directly rather than the week above, that is a fine
instruction to follow: go to your plan and start at its Phase 0.

**Exit criterion.** From a fresh install and with no curl: an admin adds a third member in Admin > Users, the one-time password is shown once, that member signs in in a second browser context and completes the forced password change; the admin then resets that member's password and disables and deletes them; no account can be created anywhere else (role='guest' is a 422 and the wizard's member step is gone) and the last active admin cannot be disabled. ### M4.7 — The box: boot, secrets, jobs, backup and the image

### M4.13 — The model basis: ledger, scoring, placement

Make a fit know the basis it was computed in - thread the bundle version, call the §10 invariant, and stop the cold half dominating the blend.

**Why these are one milestone.** ledger/, scoring/ and placement/ are one numeric stack that no surface milestone edits, and the review's version-threading theme lives almost entirely inside it: the bundle_version keyword goes on observations.standard_embeddings, the stamp goes in refit_user, and ArtifactStore.assert_matches - the §10 invariant whose own docstring claims callers it does not have - gets them at the scoring, refit and rate entrypoints. Widening "basis" from bundle to the full triple pulls in the rest: a tier-set change leaving ledger_fit at the old K, tier_edit rows keeping a raw index across a K change (whose rescale_level helper makes dd06-clamp free), the gate blend skipped for low-support titles, hyperparameters neither validated nor cached, and beta on the personal side in code and the crowd side in the corpus that measured it. ### Decision 167 — Is §5.1's β = 0.8 the crowd weight (corpus) or the personal weight (code + 0009's CHECK)?

*Gates .* **Overturns** the earlier recommendation. Confidence 0.88.

**Ruling.** The corpus reading of the *number* is right and the code's *orientation* stays. β in this codebase is the PERSONAL weight and remains so; §5.1 is amended to say that explicitly, to name the corpus's complement (β_app = 1 − β_corpus), and to correct the measured optimum in the app's own coordinates from 0.8 to 0.2. **Why.** The inversion is real and I confirmed it at the source, but the standing recommendation misdiagnoses what it costs. β is not a constant the app applies: `_cross_validate` searches the full 0..1 grid and picks per (user, kind) by held-out Spearman, using the IDENTICAL orientation as serving (foldin.py:287 vs :311/:321). ### Decision 168 — Does the K-nearest-straddler bound ship with the vectorised selector rewrite?

### Decision 170 — Should push subscribe/unsubscribe be gated behind a non-PIN session, and should SetPinRequest require the current password?

*Gates .* **Overturns** the earlier recommendation. Confidence 0.85.

**Ruling.** Gate both. `POST /api/push/subscribe` and `DELETE /api/push/subscription` move behind the same `credentialed_user` dependency as passkey registration, and `SetPinRequest` gains a required `current_password` verified exactly the way `change_password` verifies it (auth.py:216-218), in the same UPDATE that clears `pin_failed_count`/`pin_locked_until`. The rule to write down is: a PIN session reaches the product surfaces and nothing that changes how the account is reached later — and setting the PIN itself is such a change, so it costs the account's own password whatever session you are in.

**Why.** The standing recommendation rests on two cost claims and both are wrong on the evidence. Claim 1: gating push "would stop a member who lives on PIN switching from receiving §7.3 finish prompts, which no spec sentence requires." §7.3 requires the opposite of what that assumes: "when undeliverable, the prompt queues and surfaces as an in-app banner on next open", and §6's preamble guarantees an in-app equivalent for every push-carried prompt. I ran it: with zero rows in `push_subscription`, `playback.arm` then `playback.pending` returns the card ('Heat', 0.97). Gating costs the notification, never the prompt. The benefit, meanwhile, is not only security — it is a plain correctness fix. `push.py`'s upsert is `ON CONFLICT (endpoint) DO UPDATE SET user_id = EXCLUDED.user_id`, and `Onboarding.svelte:89` calls `syncSubscription()` on every mount of /account. I ran the real statement: after Patrick subscribes from his own password session and one PIN-session re-post arrives on the same endpoint, the row belongs to Jenny and Patrick has zero subscriptions. Merely opening the account page while switched silently steals the device's push channel. §3.2 scopes the PIN to "fast user-switching on a shared/TV device" — a transient identity on a shared device is exactly the identity that must not be allowed to re-bind the device's durable notification target. Unsubscribe is gated for the same reason and not out of symmetry: `disablePush()` calls the server DELETE and then `subscription.unsubscribe()`, which destroys the browser endpoint itself — so an ungated unsubscribe lets a PIN session kill a channel another member's row depends on, and cannot restore it. Claim 2: `current_password` is "redundant once credentialed_user blocks PIN sessions from /auth/pin, and adding it breaks the account page's savePin form." Redundant against the wrong threat. I enumerated the resolved dependant tree of every credential route on the real app object: `/api/auth/pin` sits behind `current_user` alone — not even `active_user` — while `/api/auth/password` verifies the stored hash and passkey registration is protected by the platform's own user-verification gesture (Face ID / Touch ID). The PIN is the only credential on this account that mints a durable, cross-device switch capability and is protected by *nothing at all*: not a password, not a platform gesture, not even the current PIN. That matters because this product hands its unlocked, credentialed device to other people by design — §3.1/§6.2 make a guest a session seat on the initiator's phone. The guest holding that phone cannot complete a passkey registration (no Face ID match) but can set a PIN on the initiator's account today, and use it from the TV next week. As for the breakage: I validated the model. The exact body savePin() posts becomes a 422 `missing ('current_password',)` — and a grep of frontend/src, e2e/ and ops/devstub.py finds exactly one call site (`account/+page.svelte:115`). The e2e suite never sets a PIN. The whole cost is one input, one binding, and six mechanical edits in test_api_gating.py — a file sec-01's own mitigation already opens. The lockout clear is not optional and the probe says so: after six wrong guesses the account carries `pin_failed_count=5` and a `pin_locked_until`; running set_pin's exact UPDATE leaves both untouched, and `check_pin` then refuses the brand-new, correct PIN with "too many attempts — try again later". Today, setting a new PIN because you were locked out does not unlock you.

**Evidence.**

- backend/spielplan/api/push.py:150-166 — `ON CONFLICT (endpoint) DO UPDATE SET user_id = EXCLUDED.user_id`; probe against a migrated schema (PGlite): endpoint owner after Patrick's credentialed subscribe = 1 (patrick); after one PIN-session re-post = 2 (jenny); `SELECT count(*) ... WHERE user_id=patrick` = 0
- frontend/src/lib/components/Onboarding.svelte:86-95 — `load()` calls `syncSubscription()` on every mount and swallows the error; frontend/src/lib/push.js:210-215 — it re-posts /push/subscribe unconditionally, so the theft needs no attacker, only a switched member opening /account
- frontend/src/lib/push.js:220-231 — `disablePush()` DELETEs the server row and then calls `subscription.unsubscribe()`, destroying the browser endpoint the other member's row also points at
- Probe over the real app object (resolved dependant tree per route): `POST /api/auth/pin -> ['current_user','set_pin']`; `POST /api/push/subscribe -> ['active_user','current_user','subscribe']`; `DELETE /api/push/subscription -> ['active_user','current_user','unsubscribe']`; `POST /api/auth/passkey/register -> ['active_user','current_user','register']`

**What changes.** Code: `deps.py` gains `credentialed_user`/`CredentialedUser` (the already-settled dependency) and `push.py`'s `subscribe` and `unsubscribe` take it instead of `ActiveUser`; `/api/push/state` stays on `ActiveUser` (reading your own device list is harmless and the onboarding screen needs it). `auth.py`: `SetPinRequest` gains `current_password: str`; `set_pin` verifies it against the stored hash with the same 401 "wrong current password" as change_password, and its UPDATE becomes `SET pin_hash = $2, pin_failed_count = 0, pin_locked_until = NULL`. Schema: none — `auth_session.auth_method` already encodes the fact; no migration, so 0016's pre-allocation is untouched. Frontend: `account/+page.svelte` gains one password input bound into `savePin()`, and hides the passkey/PIN/notification controls behind `session.user?.auth_method === 'pin'` with one line of copy (`AccountChip.svelte:22` already derives the method). Tests: extend `test_api_gating.py` with the switch-then-subscribe/unsubscribe/set-pin sweep, a 422 for a set_pin body with no `current_password` and a 401 for a wrong one, and a regression that a new PIN works immediately after a lockout; the six existing `{"pin": "4821"}` posts in that file gain `current_password`. Register them under the existing `platform-pin-profile-switch` row. No e2e change: the suite never sets a PIN. Spec: two sentences in §3.2 (below).

**Cost.** About an hour on top of the settled `credentialed_user` work, and it is nearly all mechanical. What is given up: a member who is switched into a shared device by PIN cannot turn notifications on or off there, and cannot set their PIN there — they switch back with their password or passkey first. A member who has forgotten their password can no longer set a PIN unaided; §3.1's admin-reissued one-time password is the recovery path, and this household has an admin operator. One real residual risk, named rather than hidden: a phone whose session has become a PIN session (both directions of `/auth/switch` mint `auth_method='pin'`) will silently fail to re-register if the browser later rotates its push endpoint. That is a pre-existing stale-device problem (finding fe-lc-15), not one this ruling creates, and the fix belongs with M4.11's device list.

**Spec amendment.** docs/spielplan-spec_v2.1.md, §3.2 "Authentication — passkeys first". (1) Append to the **Fallbacks** bullet: "Setting or replacing the PIN requires the account's current password, exactly as a password change does — the PIN is a credential derived from the account, not a device setting, and it is the one credential no platform gesture protects; setting it also clears any accumulated lockout, so a new PIN works immediately." ### Decision 174 — Is undo a hard delete or a tombstone, and should the fifteenth tap of a block stay undoable?

### Decision 175 — What does "a straddling title" mean, when every title on a 900-title board straddles at ±1σ?

*Gates .* **Overturns** the earlier recommendation. Confidence 0.85.

**Ruling.** Keep the ±z·σ reach — it is the right predicate and it is already one function — and fix the two things that are actually broken: the constant, and the sharing of it. `straddle_z` stays §6.3's badge constant and is retuned from 1.0 to 0.15; Tonight stops borrowing it and gets its own `BOUNDARY_Z = 0.6` beside CAP_PAIRS in tonight/round.py. Reject the posterior-mass redefinition: at the σ the model actually produces it is provably a no-op. Reject narrowing prior_var: measured, it moves nothing.

**Why.** The recommendation says 'One definition, computed in the model layer, read by rank/board.py and by tonight/round.py — otherwise the same clamp gets applied at two layers and one of them becomes a no-op.' The measurement says the opposite: sharing one σ-multiple is precisely what makes one of them a no-op, because the two surfaces measure straddling against different scales. rank/board.py compares a Ledger posterior in logit units (σ ≈ 1.2 on a real fit) against learned cutpoints spaced ≈ 1.1 apart; tonight/round.py compares a §5.1 score whose reference population is standardised to unit variance (I measure pool sd 0.87–0.94) against the rank-3/4 cut with a flat prior_var of 1.0. The same z means two different things, and I found no single value that serves both: z = 0.6 lands Tonight at a median 11 pairs — matching §6.2's own '~10 candidate votes per participant' — while still badging 97% of the board; z = 0.2 makes the badge selective at 32% but ends the round in 3 pairs, a third of what the spec designs for. On (a): option (a) is not merely weak, it is arithmetically incapable of excluding anything. For a Gaussian of sd σ centred anywhere inside a tier of width w, the maximum possible mass inside that tier is 2Φ(w/2σ) − 1. At the fitted σ = 1.2 and the observed cut gap 1.0986 that is 0.351, so P(other tier) ≥ 0.649 for EVERY title on the board, and a 0.25 threshold can never fire against it. Measured: 97.7% of the board passes it, against 98.8% for the shipped ±1σ. On (b): I swept b_i_tau from 1.0 down to 0.1 and re-fitted at each value. σ barely moves (median observed σ 1.129 → 0.700) because the (μ, v) posterior covariance dominates, not the residual prior — and the badge stays 98.5%+ universal at every value. The finding's premise that prior_var = 1.0 is 'roughly four times the real owned-pool variance' does not hold for Tonight either: the measured variance of the §5.1 score on a real pool is 0.76–0.88, so 1.0 is 1.14–1.31× it, not 4×. And that is not an accident of my fixture — it is structural. `foldin.fit_user` standardises BOTH halves of the score over the reference population, so a §5.1 score has sd ≈ 1 on the board it was standardised over, BY CONSTRUCTION. Tonight's `prior_var = 1.0` is therefore, by construction, the variance of the pool it is applied to, and `z = 1.0` means 'one whole pool standard deviation of reach'. Narrowing it to the measured variance changes 55% straddling to 55% on a 30-candidate pool and hits CAP in all 12 simulated rounds either way. That the round can never converge is confirmed rather than merely feared: on a realistic 500-candidate pool (unseen owned titles under the runtime budget — pool.py takes every scored owned title, not a top-K) 87 titles straddle at the start, and 20 answers touch at most 40 slots, so `stop_reason` can only ever return `cap` or `escape`. `converged` is dead code at z = 1.0. The queue is the strongest argument for the retune and nobody has been making it: `rank/queue.py:109` draws the 70% boundary arm from `eligible()` and `:180` draws the 20% exploration arm from the NON-straddlers (`away = [c for c in pool if c.straddle is None] or list(pool)`). At z = 1.0 the boundary arm draws from 824 of 900 titles and the exploration arm from 76 — two arms that are the same arm. At z = 0.15 the split is 257/643, which is what 'boundary-targeted active selection' means. Where I stop short of the owner: the exact badge z is partly a taste call about how many titles should wear an A/S chip on a two-person board, and I give the dial rather than pretending otherwise — z 0.3 → 69%, 0.2 → 32%, 0.15 → 24%, 0.1 → 16%.
